Canto 6 — Dvārakā-khaṇḍa

Chapter 1 - Jarāsandha’s Defeat

śrī-nārada uvāca
kṛṣṇāya vāsudevāya
devakī-nandanāya ca
nanda-gopa-kumarāya
govindāya namo namaḥ 6.1.1

Śrī Nārada said: “I offer my respectful obeisances to Lord Kṛṣṇa, the son of Vasudeva, Devakī, and the gopa Nanda, and the happiness of the cows, land, and senses.”

śrutaṁ tava mukhād brahman
mathurā-khaṇḍam adbhutam
vada māṁ dvārakā-khaṇḍaṁ
śrī-kṛṣṇa-caritāmṛtam 6.1.2

“O brāhmaṇa, now that I have heard from your mouth the wonderful Mathurā-khaṇḍa, please tell me the Dvārakā-khaṇḍa, which is filled with the nectar of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental pastimes.”

vivāhāḥ kati putrāś ca
kati pautrā ramā-pateḥ
sarvaṁ vada mahā-buddhe
dvārakā-vāsa-kāraṇam 6.1.3

“How many wives did He have? How many children? How many grandchildren? Tell me the reason He chose to live in Dvārakā. O wise one, please tell me everything.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
asti-prāptī mahiṣyau dve
mṛte kaṁse mahā-bale
jarāsandha-gṛhaṁ duḥkhāj
jagmatur maithileśvara 6.1.4

Śrī Nārada said: “O king of Mithilā, after the death of powerful Kaṁsa, his two queens, Asti and Prāpti unhappily went to Jarāsandha’s home.”

tan-mukhāt kaṁsa-maraṇaṁ
śrutvā kruddho jara-sūtaḥ
ayādavīṁ mahīṁ kartuṁ
udyato ’bhūn mahā-balaḥ 6.1.5

“When from their mouths he heard of Kaṁsa’s death, Jarāsandha becxame angry and decided to make the earth a place where no Yādavas lived.”

akṣauhiṇībhir viṁśatyā
tisrbhiś cāpi samvṛtaḥ
ramyāṁ mahā-purīṁ rājann
āyayau balavān nṛpaḥ 6.1.6

“Accompanied by 23 akṣauhiṇī divisions, powerful King Jarāsandha went to beautiful Mathurā City.”

bhayāturāṁ purīṁ vīkṣya
tat-senāṁ sindhu-nādinīm
sabhāyāṁ bhagavān sākṣād
baladevam uvāca ha 6.1.7

“Seeing that the entire city was terrified of that great army roaring like the ocean, in the royal assembly Lord Kṛṣṇa said to Balarāma:”

sarvaṁ cāsya balaṁ rāma
hantavyaṁ vai na saṁśayaḥ
māgadhas tu na hantavyo
bhūyaḥ kartā balodyamam 6.1.8

“O Balarāma, this army should be destroyed, but Jarāsandha should not be killed. Of this there is no doubt.”

jarāsandha-nimittena
bharaṁ vai bhubhujāṁ bhuvaḥ
sarvaṁ cātra hariṣyāmi
kariṣyāmi priyaṁ satām 6.1.9

“I will remove the great burden of demonic kings Jarāsandha has placed on this earth. In this way I will please the devotees.”

evaṁ vadati kṛṣṇe vai
vaikuṇṭhāc ca rathau śubhau
abhūtām āgatau rājan
sarveṣāṁ paśyatāṁ ca tau 6.1.10

“O king, as Lord Kṛṣṇa spoke these words two beautiful chariots arrived from Vaikuṇṭhaloka as everyone watched.”

samāruhya rathau sādyo
rāma-kṛṣṇau mahā-balau
yādavānāṁ balaiḥ sūkṣmais
tvaraṁ nirjagmatuḥ purāt 6.1.11

“Mounting these chariots, and accompanied by a small Yādava army, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma at once left the city.”

yādavānāṁ māgadhānāṁ
paśyadbhir divijair divi
babhūva tumulaṁ yuddhaṁ
adbhutaṁ roma-harṣaṇam 6.1.12

“As the demigods in heaven watched, the Yādavas and Māgadhas fought a tumultuous battle that made the hairs of the body stand erect with wonder.”

akṣauhiṇībhir daśabhī
rathārūḍho mahā-balaḥ
śrī-kṛṣṇasya puraḥ pūrvaṁ
yuyudhe māgadheśvaraḥ 6.1.13

“Riding on a chariot, and accompanied by ten akṣauhiṇī divisons, Jarāsandha fought with Lord Kṛṣṇa.”

pañcabhiś cākṣauhiṇībhir
dhāṛṭarāṣṭraḥ suyodhanaḥ
yuyodha yādavaiḥ sārdhaṁ
jarāsandha-sahāya-kṛt 6.1.14

“Accompanied by five akṣauhiṇī divisons, Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s son Duryodhana also fought on jarāsandha’s side against the Yādavas.”

pañcabhiś ca tathā rājan
vindhyā-deśādhipo balī
tisrbhiś ca mahā-yuddhe
baṅga-nātho mahā-balaḥ 6.1.15

“O king, accompanied by five akṣauhiṇīs, the powerful king of Vindhyā-deśa fought on Jarāsandha’s side. Accompanied by three akṣauhiṇīs, the powerful king of Bengal also fought on Jarāsandha’s side.”

evam anye ’pi rājāno
jarāsandha-vaśānugāḥ
prāṇaiḥ sahāyaṁ kurvantau
jarāsandhasya maithila 6.1.16

“O king of Mithilā, many other kings, Jarāsandha’s vassals, gave their lives to help him in the battle.”

bāṇāndhakāre sañjāte
śatru-senā-samākule
ṭāṅkaraṁ śārṅga-dhanuṣaḥ
śārṅga-dhanvā cakāra ha 6.1.17

“When the enemy armies darkened the sky with a shower of arrows, Lord Kṛṣṇa filled the sky with the twanging sounds of His Śārṅga bow.”

nanāda tena brahmāṇḍaṁ
sapta-lokair bilaiḥ saha
vicelur dig-gajās tārā
ejad-bhū-khaṇḍa-maṇḍalam 6.1.18

“The entire universe, with the seven planetary systems and the vast regions of outer space, echoed with that sound. The earth, the stars, and the elephants that hold up the directions all trembled.”

tadaiva badhirī-bhūtaṁ
śatrūṇāṁ sainya-maṇḍalam
utpatanto hāya yuddhād
gajās tu vimukhās tataḥ 6.1.19

“Then there was a deafening noise in the circle of the enemy armies, and the horses and elephants fled the battlefield.”

dudrāva tad-balaṁ sarvaṁ
ṭāṅkarād bhaya-vihvalam
pratīpam etya gavyūtiḥ
punas tatrājagāma ha 6.1.20

“When it heard the twanging of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s bow, Jarāsandha’s army became terrified and ran two miles from the battlefield.”

evaṁ śārṅgaṁ samuccārya
taḍit-piṅga-sphurat-prabham
bāṇaughaś chādayām āsa
jarāsandha-balaṁ hariḥ 6.1.21

“Then, lifting His Śārṅga bow glistening like lightning, Lord Kṛṣṇa unleased a flood of arrows that made a great shadow over Jarāsandha’s army.”

cūrṇī-bhūtā rathā rājan
bāṇaughaiḥ śārṅga-dhanvanaḥ
cūrṇa-cakrā nipetuḥ kau
hata-sūtāś ca nāyakāḥ 6.1.22

“O king, the flood of arrows from the Śārṅga bow broke the chariots into pieces. Their wheels broken, the chariots crashed to the ground and the warriors and charioteers on them all died.”

dvidha-bhūtā gajā bāṇais
calitā gajibhiḥ saha
sāśva-vāhās tathāsvāś ca
bāṇaiḥ sañchinna-kandharāḥ 6.1.23

“The many arrows cut the elephants and their riders into two pieces and severed the heads of the horses and horsemen.”

tathā vīrā mahā-yuddhe
bhinnorāś chinna-mastakāḥ
viśīrṇa-kavacāḥ petur
bāṇaughaiś chinna-saṁśayāḥ 6.1.24

“Their armor broken, chests wounded, and heads severed by the flood of arrows, the warriors in that great batlle fell, their hopes shattered.”

adho-mukhā ūrdhva-mukhās
chinna-dehā nṛpātmajāḥ
rejū raṇāṅgane rājan
bhāṇḍa-vyūhā ivāhatāḥ 6.1.25

“O king, their faces up or down and their bodies broken, the princes littered the battlefield like so many broken earthen pots.”

kṣaṇa-mātreṇa tad-yuddhe
śata-krośa-vilambitā
āpagābhūn mahā-durgā
rudhira-srava-sambhavāḥ 6.1.26

“In that battle there was for two-hundred miles a flowing river of blood, . . .”

dvipa-grāhā coṣṭra-khara-
kabandhāśvādi-kacchapā
śiśumāra-rathā keśa-
śaivalā bhuja-sarpiṇī 6.1.27

“. . . where the elephants were crocodiles, the camels, mules, horses, and headless corpses giant turtles, the chariots porpoises, the severed arms snakes, . . .”

kara-mīnā mauli-ratna-
hāra-kuṇḍala-śarkarā
śastra-śuktiś chinna-śaṅkhā
cāmara-dhvaja-saikatā 6.1.28

“. . . the severed hands fish, the helmets, earrings, and jewel necklaces pebbles, the weapons oysters, the severed heads cāmaras and flags on the sandy shore, . . .”

rathāṅgāvarta-samyuktā
senā-dvaya-taṭāvṛtā
śata-yojana-vistīrṇā
babhau vaitāraṇī yathā 6.1.29

“. . . and the chariot wheels whirlpools. The two armies its shores, that river flowed for eight hundred miles like the river Vaitaraṇī in the hellish worlds.”

pramathā bhairavā bhūtā
veṭalā yoginī-gaṇāḥ
aṭṭa-hāsaṁ prakurvanto
nṛtyanto raṇa-maṇḍale 6.1.30

“Laughing uproariously, many goblins, ghosts, bhairavas, veṭalās and yoginīs danced in the battlefield.”

pibanto rudhiraṁ śaśvat
kapālena nṛpeśvara
harasya muṇḍa-mālārthaṁ
jagṛhus te sirāṁsi ca 6.1.31

“O king of kings, again and again they drank the blood of the dead. They gathered many skulls for Lord Śiva’s garland of skulls.”

siṁhārūḍhā bhadrakālī
ḍākinī-śata-samvṛtā
pibantī rudhiraṁ coṣṇaṁ
sāṭṭa-hāsaṁ cakāra ha 6.1.32

“Riding on a lion, and accompanied a hundred female goblins, goddess Bhadrakālī drank the warm blood of the dead and laughed uproariously.”

vidyādharyaś ca svarga-sthā
gandharvyo ’psarasas tathā
kṣatra-dharma-sthitān vīrān
vavrire deva-rūpiṇaḥ 6.1.33

“The warriors that died nobly upholding the principles of kṣatriya-dharma became demigods and the Vidyādharī, Gandharvīs, and Apsarā demigoddesses of Svargaloka selected them to be their husbands.”

gṛhitvā tān kalir abhūt
tāsāṁ paty-artham ambare
mamānurūpā tenaiva
iti tad-gata-cetasām 6.1.34

“Many demigoddess desiring him as her husband, they quarreled over each hero. They each thought, “He is perfect for me.”

kecid vīrā dharma-parā
raṇa-raṅgān na calitāḥ
yayur viṣṇu-padaṁ divyaṁ
bhittvā mārtaṇḍa-maṇḍalam 6.1.35

“Some heroes, staunchly devoted to duty and not fleeing the battlefield, passed through the circle of the sun and went to the transcendental abode of Lord Viṣṇu.”

śeṣaṁ balaṁ samākṛṣya
baladevo halena vai
muśalenāhanat kruddhas
trailokya-bala-dhārakaḥ 6.1.36

“Possessing all the strength in the three worlds, angry Lord Balarāma killed all the remaining soldiers with His club.”

evaṁ sainye kṣayaṁ yāte
jarāsandhasya sarvataḥ
suyodhano vindhyā-nātho
baṅga-nāthas tathaiva ca
sarve vidudruvur yuddhād
bhaya-bhītā itas tataḥ
jarāsandho mahā-vīryo
nāgāyuta-samo bale
rathenāgatavān rājan
baladevasya sammukhe
samākṛṣya halāgreṇa
jarāsandha-rathaṁ śubham 6.1.37-39

“When Jarāsandha’s army was completely destroyed, the king of Vindhyā-deśa, the king of Bengal, and Duryodhana became terrified and fled here and there. Then Jarāsandha, who was as strong as ten thousand elephants, drove his chariot to meet Lord Balarāma. With the tip of His plow Lord Balarāma caught the beautiful chariot and dragged it here and there.”

cūrṇayām āsa sahasā
musalena yadūttamaḥ
jarāsandho ’pi viratho
hatāśvo hata-sārathiḥ 6.1.40

“Then Lord Balarāma, the best of the Yādavas, broke the chariot to pieces. Now Jarāsandha had no chariot and his horses and charioteer had been killed.”

jagrāha balinaṁ dorbhyāṁ
santyaktvā śastra-saṁhitam
tayor yuddham abhūd ghoraṁ
bāhūbhyāṁ raṇa-maṇḍale 6.1.41

“Then Lord Balarāma threw away His weapons and with both hands grabbed powerful Jarāsandha. Hand-to-hand they fought a terrible duel in the battlefield.”

paśyatāṁ divi devānāṁ
narāṇāṁ bhuvi maithila
urasā śirasā caiva
bāhūbhyāṁ pādayoḥ pṛthak
yuyudhāte malla-yuddhe
siṁhāv iva mahā-bale
tayoś ca yuddhyatoḥ sarvaṁ
kṣuṇṇaṁ bhū-khaṇḍa-maṇḍalam 6.1.42-43

“O king of Mithilā, as the demigods in heaven and the humans on earth watched, Lord Balarāma and Jarāsandha wrestled arm-to-arm, foot-to-foot, chest-to-chest, and head-to-head, like two very powerful lions. As they fought the circle of the earth became crushed and broken.”

sthalīva sahasā rājaṁs
cakampe ghaṭikā-dvayam
gṛhītvā bhuja-daṇḍābhyāṁ
jarāsandhaṁ yadūttamaḥ
bhū-pṛṣṭhe pothayām āsa
kamāṇḍalum ivārbhakaḥ
rāmas tad-upari sthitvā
hantuṁ śatruṁ jarā-sutam
jagrāha musalaṁ ghoraṁ
krodha-pūrita-vigrahaḥ
paripūrṇatamenātha
śrī-kṛṣṇena mahātmanā
nivāritas tadaivāśu
taṁ mumoca yadūttamaḥ
tapase kṛta-saṅkalpo
vrīḍito ’pi jarā-sutaḥ 6.1.44-47

“O king, for an hour the earth shook. Then Lord Balarāma, the best of the Yādavas, His transcendental body filled with anger, grabbed Jarāsandha with both hands, threw him to the ground as a child throws a kamaṇḍalu, jumped on him, grabbed a terrible club, and was about to kill him, when Lord Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, stopped Him. Lord Balarāma at once released him. Now Jarāsandha was humiliated and his heart was filled with pain.”

nivārito mantri-mukhyair
māgadhān māgadho yayau
itthaṁ jitvā jarāsandhaṁ
mādhavo madhusūdanaḥ
āyodhana-gataṁ vittaṁ
sarvaṁ nītvā sukhāvaham
yādavān agrataḥ kṛtvā
baladeva-samanvitaḥ
upagīyamāna-vijayaḥ
suta-māgadha-vandibhiḥ
śaṅkha-dundubhi-nādena
brahma-ghoṣeṇa bhūyasā
viveśa mathurāṁ sākṣāt
paripūrṇatamaḥ svayam 6.1.48-51

“Advised by his counselors, Jarāsandha returned to Magadha-deśa. In this way Lord Kṛṣṇa, the perfect Supreme Personality of Godhead, the husband of the goddess of fortune and the killer of the Madhu demon, defeated Jarāsandha, took all the wealth plundered in the fight, placed it before the Yādavas, and, His glories proclaimed by the Sūtas, Māgadhas, and Vandīs, and by the chanting of the Vedas and the sounds of the conchshells and drums, in Lord Balarāma’s company entered Mathurā City.”

Chapter 2 - Residence in Śrī Dvārakā

punas tatra jarāsandhas
tāv utyākṣauhiṇī-balaḥ
yuyudhe yadubhiḥ śīghraṁ
punaḥ kṛṣṇa-parājitaḥ 6.2.1

“Again Jarāsandha approached Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma with an army of many akṣauhiṇīs, again he fought with the Yādavas, and again Lord Kṛṣṇa quickly defeated him.”

śrī-kṛṣṇa-tejasā sarve
yādavā vṛddhim āgatāḥ
dhanur-gajādibhiḥ śaśvat
prāpta-luṇṭhana-sāhasaḥ 6.2.2

“The Yādavas again and again plundered great wealth and many bows, elephants, and other things from the armies defeated by Lord Kṛṣṇa transcendental power.”

prāpte ca sahasā rājan
vinā yuddhaṁ puraiva hi
arbhakā jala-hāriṇyas
cakruḥ śatrv-apahāraṇam 6.2.3

“O king, without having to fight, the little girls carrying water to the Yādava soldiers could easily take what they wanted from the slain soldiers of the enemy.”

śatru-dravyaṁ ca saṁhartuṁ
vīkṣantaḥ krīta-vāsasaḥ
nāgarā māthurāḥ sarve
paraṁ harṣam upāgatāḥ 6.2.4

“Seeing the valuable garments and property of the enemy soldiers, the people of Mathurā became very happy.”

evaṁ sapta-daśa kṛtvā
kṣīṇa-sainyo jarā-sutaḥ
aṣṭa-daśama-saṅgrāme
āgantuṁ ca mano ’karot 6.2.5

“Even though his army had been destroyed seventeen times in this way, Jarāsandha still fixed his mind on an eighteenth war.”

mayā praṇoditaḥ kāla-
yavano vai mahā-balaḥ
rurodha mathurāṁ kruddho
mleccha-koṭi-samāvṛtaḥ 6.2.6

“Then, encouraged by me, powerful King Kālayavana beseiged Mathurā with an army of ten million mlecchas.”

mlecchānāṁ ca balaṁ vīkṣya
sva-puraṁ bhaya-vihvalam
bhayaṁ cobhayataḥ prāptaṁ
rāmeṇācintayad dhariḥ 6.2.7

“Seeing that their city was terrified of the mleccha army, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma considered what to do.”

sva-jñāti-bandhu-rakṣārthaṁ
samudre bhīma-nādini
cakāra dvārakā-durgaṁ
eka-ratreṇa mādhavaḥ 6.2.8

“Then, in one night Lord Kṛṣṇa built the Dvārakā fort in the middle of the ocean filled with fearful roaring sounds.”

yatrāṣṭa-dik-pāla-siddhir
viśvakarma-vinirmitā
sarvā vaikuṇṭha-sampattir
dṛśyate mokṣa-kaṅkṣibhiḥ 6.2.9

“Viśvakarmā arranged that all eight directions were perfectly protected. They who yearn for liberatuion could see that city had all the opulences of Vaikuṇṭha.”

hariḥ sarva-janaṁ tatra
nītvā yogena maithila
purād rāmam anujñāpya
nirgato ’bhūn nirāyudhaḥ 6.2.10

“By His mystic power Lord Kṛṣṇa then took all the people of Mathurā to Dvārakā. O king of Mithilā, after taking His leave of Lord Balarāma, Lord Kṛṣṇa, now unarmed, went outside the Mathurā City walls.”

nirāyudhaṁ hariṁ jñātvā
mayoktair lakṣaṇaiḥ khalaḥ
nirayudhaḥ sa taṁ yoddhuṁ
padātiḥ svayam āgataḥ 6.2.11

“Aware, by the description I had given him, that this unarmed person was Kṛṣṇa, the demon put down his arms and walked to Kṛṣṇa to start a fight.”

parāṅ-mukhaṁ pradravantaṁ
durāpaṁ yoginām api
jighṛkṣus taṁ cānvadhāvat
sainikānāṁ prapaśyatām 6.2.12

“Then Kṛṣṇa turned and fled. As the host of soldiers watched, Kālayavana ran in pursuit of Lord Kṛṣṇa, whom even the great yogīs cannot catch.”

hasta-prāptaṁ vapus tasmai
darśayann iva mādhavaḥ
dūraṁ gataḥ śyāmalādreḥ
prāviśat kandaraṁ tvaram 6.2.13

“Seeming only a hand’s legnth away, Lord Kṛṣṇa ran a great distance and finally entered the cave of a black mountain.”

mucukundo yatra cāste
mandhātṛ-tanayo mahān
asurebhyaḥ purā rakṣāṁ
devānāṁ yaś cakāra ha 6.2.14

“In that cave stayed King Mandhātā noble son Mucukunda, who in ancient times protected the demigods from the demons.”

ahar-niśaṁ na suṣvāpa
deva-senā-paro nṛpa
tam ūcur devatāḥ sarve
prasannā rāja-sattamam 6.2.15

O king, as the commander-in-chief of the demigods’ armies, he had not slept for many days and nights. Pleased with him, all the demigods finally approached that great king and said:”

varaṁ varāya bho rājan
yat te manasi vartate
natvā tān prāha rājendraḥ
karomi śayanaṁ param 6.2.16

“O king, please ask for a boon. Ask for whatever is in your heart.” Bowing down to them, that king of kings said: “I wish to sleep a great sleep.”

śayanānte hareḥ sākṣād-
darśanaṁ me bhavatv alam
yo madhye bodhayen māṁ vai
śayanaṁ tv apy acetanam
sa mayā dṛṣṭa-matras tu
bhāsmī-bhavatu tat-kṣaṇāt
tathā ca coktaḥ suṣvāpa
rājā kṛta-yuge purā 6.2.17-18

“When my sleep is over I will see Lord Kṛṣṇa directly. Anyone who wakens me before that, while I am still sleeping, I will burn to ashes with a glance.” After speaking these words in ancient times, in the Satya-yuga, King Mucukunda went to sleep.”

tatra praviṣṭo yavano
matvā pītāmbaraṁ ca tam
tatāḍa yavanaḥ kruddhaḥ
padenāśu mahā-khalaḥ 6.2.19

“Entering that cave, the great demon Kālayavana at once kicked King Mucukunda, thinking him to be Lord Kṛṣṇa,”

mucukundaḥ samutthāya
śanair unmilya so ’kṣinī
āśāḥ prapaśyaṁs taṁ pārśve
sthitaṁ kālaṁ dadarśa ha 6.2.20

“Slowly opening his eyes, Mucukunda became awake. Looking in all directions, he finally saw Kālayavana standing at his side.”

sa tāvat tasya ruṣṭasya
dṛṣṭi-pātena maithila
dehajenāgninā dagdho
bhāsmasād abhavat kṣaṇāt 6.2.21

“O king of Mithilā, then King Mucukunda became angry and with a single glance burned to demon to ashes in a moment.”

bhāsmī-bhūte ca yavane
paripūrṇatamaḥ svayam
svarūpaṁ darśayām āsa
mucukundāya dhīmate 6.2.22

“When Kālayavana was thus burned to ashes, the original Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Kṛṣṇa, showed His own form to wise Mucukunda, a form that . . .”

koṭi-sūrya-pratīkāśe
jyotiṣāṁ maṇḍale prabhum
sthitaṁ sphuṭat-kirīṭārkaṁ
kuṇḍalāṅgada-nūpuram 6.2.23

“. . . was splendid as ten million suns, circled with light, all-powerful, decorated with a crown splendid as the sun, decorated with earrings, armlets, and anklets, . . .”

śrīvatsāṅkaṁ catur-bāhuṁ
padmākṣaṁ vana-mālinam
koṭi-kandarpa-lavaṇaṁ
kāla-megha-sama-prabham 6.2.24

“. . . had lotus eyes and four arms, was marked with Śrīvatsa and decorated with a forest garland, was splendid like a black cloud, and was more handsome than ten million Kāmadevas.”

dṛṣṭvā rājā harṣito ’pi
samutthāya kṛtāñjaliḥ
paripūrṇatamaṁ jñātvā
bhaktyā taṁ praṇanāma ha 6.2.25

“Gazing at this form and aware that this was the perfect Supreme Personality of Godhead, King Mucukunda became filled with happiness. First he stood with folded hands and then He bowed before the Lord with great devotion.”

śrī-mucukunda uvāca
kṛṣṇāya vasudevāya
devakī-nandanāya ca
nanda-gopa-kumarāya
govindāya namo namaḥ 6.2.26

Śrī Mucukunda said: “Let me offer my respectful obeisances unto the Lord, who has become the son of Vasudeva, the pleasure of Devakī, the boy of Nanda and the other cowherd men of Vṛndāvana, and the enlivener of the cows and the senses.”

namaḥ paṅkaja-nābhāya
namaḥ paṅkaja-māline
namaḥ paṅkaja-netrāya
namas te paṅkajāṅghraye 6.2.27

“My respectful obeisances are unto you, O Lord, whose abdomen is marked with a dpression like a lotus flower, who are always decorated with garlands of lotus flowers, whose glance is as cool as the lotus, and whose feet are engraved with lotuses.”

namaḥ kṛṣṇāya suddhāya
brahmaṇe paramātmane
praṇata-kleśa-nāśāya
govindāya namo namaḥ 6.2.28

“My respectful obeisances are unto supremely pure Lord Kṛṣṇa, who is both the impersonal Brahman and the all-pervading Supersoul, who is the enlivener of the cows and the senses, and who destroys the sufferings of the surrendered devotees.”

namo ’stv anantāya sahasra-mūrtaye
sahasra-pādākṣi-śiroru-bāhave
sahasra-nāmne puruṣāya śāśvate
sahasra-koṭī-yuga-dhāriṇe namaḥ 6.2.29

“My respectful obeisances are unto limitless Lord Kṛṣṇa, who appears in millions of forms, who has millions of feet, eyes, heads, thighs, and arms, who has millions of names, who is the eternal Supreme Person, and who maintains the worlds for billions of yugas.”

hare mat-samaḥ patākī nāsti bhūmau
tathā tvat-samo nāsti pāpāpahārī
iti tvaṁ ca matvā jagannātha deva
yathecchā bhavet te tathā māṁ kuru tvam 6.2.30

“O Lord Kṛṣṇa, in this world no one is as sinful as I and no one is as purifying as You. Therefore, O master of the worlds, O effulgent Lord, please consider my case and do what You wish.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
evaṁ stuto hariḥ sākṣāt
paramānanda-vigrahaḥ
jñātvā taṁ nirguṇāṁ bhaktaṁ
prāha gambhirayā girā 6.2.31

Śrī Nārada said: “Hearing these prayers, and aware that Mucukunda’s devotion was beyond the touch of the mades of nature, Lord Kṛṣṇa, whose form is filled with transcendental bliss, spoke with a deep voice.”

śrī-bhagavān uvāca
dhanyas tvaṁ rāja-śardūla
dhanyā te vimalā matiḥ
nairapekṣyena divyena
bhakti-bhāvena pūritā 6.2.32

The Blessed Lord said: “O tiger of kings, you are fortunate. Your pure thoughts are glorious. They are filled with perfect renunciation and splendid transcendental love.”

adyaiva gaccha mad-dhāma
badary-ākhyaṁ mad-āśrayaḥ
tatraiva tu tapas taptvā
bhūtvā brāhmaṇa-puṅgavaḥ 6.2.33

“Go at once to My abode named Badarikāśrama. By performing austerities there you will become the best of brāhmaṇas.”

prema-lakṣaṇayā bhaktyā
mad-dhāma prakṛteḥ param
prāpsyasi tvaṁ mahā-rāja
yato nāvartate gataḥ 6.2.34

“O great king, by serving Me with loving devotion You will attain My own abode, which is above the world of matter. They who go there never return to the world of birth and death.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
itthaṁ stutvā hariṁ natvā
parikramya natānanaḥ
niścakrāma guhā-durgāc
chrī-kṛṣṇa-prema-vihvalaḥ 6.2.35

Śrī Nārada said: “After offering prayers, bowing down, and with bowed head circumambulating the Lord, King Mucukunda, now overwhelmed with love for Lord Kṛṣṇa, left the mountain cave.”

dvāpare kṣullakā martyā
tāla-vrkṣa-śatocchritam
dṛṣṭvā taṁ dudruvur mārge
bhaya-bhītā itas tataḥ 6.2.36

“The Dvāpara-yuga people, who were all very short, became terrified and fled when on the road they saw King Mucukunda, who was tall as a hundred palm trees.”

mā bhaiṣṭety abhayaṁ yacchan
jagāma diśam uttaram
evaṁ dattvā varaṁ tasmai
mucukundāya dhīmate
bhagavān punar āvrajya
mathurāṁ mleccha-veṣṭitām
hatvā mleccha-balaṁ sarvaṁ
tad-dhanāny acchinad balāt 6.2.37-38

“Saying, “Don’t be afraid”, Mucukunda removed their fears and continued north. Then Lord Kṛṣṇa, after giving His blessings to wise Mucukunda, returned to Mathurā City, which was surrounded by the mlecchas. The Lord killed the entire mleccha army and forcibly broke the mlecchas’ power.”

atha rājā jarāsandho
yoddhum abhyuditaḥ punaḥ
āhūya māgadhān viprān
muhūrtādeśa-kāriṇaḥ 6.2.39

“Then King Jarāsandha, eager to fight again with Lord Kṛṣṇa, called together the great astrologer-brāhmaṇas of the Māgadha country.”

prāhedaṁ vāsudevākhyaṁ
jitvā yady āgato hy aham
sarvān sampūjayiṣyāmi
sadā yuṣmat-padāśraye 6.2.40

“Jarāsandha said to them: If I defeat Kṛṣṇa, when I return I will take shelter of your feet and worship you all forever.”

kārāgāreṣu yāvad vai
sthitā bhavata bho dvijāḥ
parājito ’haṁ va yuṣmān
haniṣyāmi na saṁśayaḥ 6.2.41

“But if I am defeated and imprisoned, I will kill you all. Of this there is no doubt.”

evam uktvā dvijān rājā
jarāsandho mahā-balaḥ
ājagāmāśu mathurāṁ
trayo-viṁśaty-anīka-paḥ 6.2.42

“After speaking these words, powerful King Jarāsandha, taking with him 23 army divisions, quickly went to Mathurā City.”

brahma-vākyam ṛtaṁ kartuṁ
sva-pratijñāṁ vihāya ca
manuṣya-ceṣṭām āpannau
sva-purād bhīta-bhītavat
rāma-kṛṣṇau parau devau
padbhyāṁ dudruvatur drutam
palāyamānau tau vīkṣya
māgadhaḥ prahasan bhṛśam
anvadhāvad rathānīkair
brahma-vākyam anusmaran
dakṣināśaṁ gatāv itthaṁ
pravarṣaṇa-girau harī 6.2.43-45

“Seeing Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma fleeing on foot as if They were ordinary terrified human beings, Jarāsandha laughed aloud. At first he was determined not to keep the promise he made to the brāhmaṇas. Then, with many chariot warriors pursuing Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma as They ran south to Mount Pravarṣaṇa, he remembered his words to the brāhmaṇas.”

yasmin nilīnau jñātvā tāv
edhobhis taṁ dadāha ha
bhāsmī-bhūte vane jāte
dahyamāna-tata-gireḥ 6.2.46

“Aware that Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma were hiding in the mountain, Jarāsandha set the mountain on fire and burned all its forests to ashes.”

daśaika-yojanottuṅgāt
samutpatya sureśvarau
alakṣyamanāv aribhir
dvārakāyaṁ nipetatuḥ 6.2.47

“Jumping from a height of eighty-eight miles, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, the two Supreme Personalities of Godhead, unseen by Their enemies, landed in Dvārakā.”

so ’pi dagdhau ca tau matvā
māgadhendro mahā-balaḥ
māgadhān prayayau vīro
vādayan jaya-dundubhīn 6.2.48

“Thinking that Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma had been burned to death, powerful Jarāsandha returned to the country of Māgadha, where he was a hero and the drums of victory sounded.”

Chapter 3 - Lord Balarāma’s Wedding

śrī-nārada uvāca
itthaṁ mayā te kathitaṁ
dvārakā-vāsa-kāraṇam
vivāhādi-kathāḥ sarvā
vadiṣyāmi pareśayoḥ 6.3.1

“In this way I have described to you the reason the Lord lives in Dvārakā. Now I will tell you everything of the marriages of Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, the two Supreme Personalities of Godhead.”

pūrvaṁ śrī-baladevasya
vivāhaṁ śṛṇu maithila
sarva-pāpa-haraṁ puṇyaṁ
āyur-vardhanam uttamam 6.3.2

“O king of Mithilā, please hear the story of Lord Balarāma’s wedding, a sacred and transcendental story that removes all sins and increases the span of one’s life.”

ānarto nāma rājābhūt
sūrya-vaṁse mahā-manāḥ
yan-nāmnānarta-deśaḥ syāt
samudre bhīma-nādini 6.3.3

“A noble-hearted king named Ānarta was born in the sūrya dynasty. The country of Ānarta-deśa, which was created where there once was only the ocean filled with terrible sounds, was named after him.”

raivato nāma tat-putras
cakravartī guṇākaraḥ
rājyaṁ cakāra sa purīṁ
vinirmāya kuśasthalīm 6.3.4

“His son was the virtuous king Raivata, who ruled the kingdom from his capitol Kuśasthalī.”

tasya putra-śataṁ cāsīd
revatī nāma kanyakā
sarvottamaṁ cirañjīvaṁ
sundaraṁ varam icchatī 6.3.5

“He had a hundred sons and one daughter. His daughter, who was named Revatī, yearned to have a husband that was handsome, lived eternally, and was better than all other men.”

ekadā ratham āsthāya
hema-ratna-vibhūṣitam
āropya svaṁ duhitaraṁ
raivataḥ paryaṭan bhuvam 6.3.6

“One day he placed his daughter on a chariot decorated with gold and jewels, and wandered the earth to find a proper husband.”

prāpto yoga-balenāpi
brahmalokaṁ śubhāvaham
kanyā-varaṁ paripraṣṭuṁ
brahmāṇaṁ praṇanāma ha 6.3.7

“By his mystic power he traveled to Brahmaloka. His intention to ask for a proper husband for his daughter, he bowed before the demigod Brahmā”

gāyantyāṁ pūrva-cittyāṁ ca
sthito labdha-kṣaṇaḥ kṣaṇam
eka-cittaṁ vidhiṁ jñātvā
svābhiprāyaṁ nyavedayat 6.3.8

“As the apsarā Pūrvacitti was singing, he found his opportunity. Aware that now he had Brahmā’s attention, he spoke what was in his heart.”

śrī-raivata uvāca
paraḥ purāṇo jagad-aṅkuro ’bhūḥ
pūrṇaḥ parātmā parameśvaro ’si
sthitaḥ sadā dhāmani pārameṣṭhya
sṛjasy alaṁ pāsi ca hiṁsasīdam 6.3.9

Śrī Raivata said: “You are the greatest, the oldest, the seed from which this universe has sprouted, the great soul and the great controller. O Brahmā, you stay always in your own abode. You create, maintain, and destroy this universe.”

vedā mukhaṁ dharma uras tathaiva
pṛṣṭhaṁ hy adharmaś ca manur manīṣā
aṅgāni devā asurāś ca pādāḥ
sarvā sṛtir deva tanus tava syāt 6.3.10

“O lord, the Vedas are Your mouth, religion is your chest, irreligion is your back, Manu is your intelligence, the demigods are your limbs, the demons are your feet, and this entire creation is your body.”

karoṣi hastāmalakaṁ ca viśvaṁ
netuṁ prabhuḥ sārathivad guṇeṣu
ekas tvam ekaṁ ca vidhāya jālaṁ
grasiṣyase sarvam ivorṇanābhiḥ 6.3.11

“This universe is like a small āmalaka fruit in your hand. You are like a charioteer driving this universe through the modes of nature. You are like a spider and this universe is your web. In the future you will swallow up this entire universe.”

mahendra-dhiṣṇyaṁ tava vaśyam asti
kiṁ sārvabhaumaṁ kim u yoga-siddhiḥ
yaḥ pārameṣṭhyaṁ ca sadā sthito ’si
tasmai namo ’nanta-guṇāya bhūmne 6.3.12

“The realm of King Indra is under your control, what to speak of the realms of ordinary kings or the perfections of yoga. You always stay in the highest realm. To you, O lord of limitless virtue, I offer my respectful obeisances.”

bhavān svayambhūr jagatāṁ pitāmaho
vidhe sura-jyeṣṭha iti prabhāvataḥ
asya varaṁ sarva-guṇaṁ cirāyuṣaṁ
vadāśu māṁ divyam aśeṣa-darśanaḥ 6.3.13

“You are self-born. You are the grandfather of all in the universe. You are the best of the demigods. You see everything. O lord, please tell me who should become my daughter’s husband, a divine husband that lives forever and has all virtues.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
etac chrutvā tato brahmā
svayambhūḥ sarva-darśanaḥ
raivataṁ prāha rājānaṁ
prahasann iva maithila 6.3.14

Śrī Nārada said: “O king of Mithilā, when he heard these words, self-born Brahmā, who sees everything, smiled and spoke to King Raivata.”

śrī-brahmovāca
atra kṣaṇena he rājan
bhuvi kālo mahā-balī
tvaraṁ vyatitas tri-nava-
catur-yuga-vikalpitaḥ 6.3.15

Śrī Brahmā said: “O king, a moment in this realm of Brahmaloka is equal to four yugas on the earth.”

na santi martya-loke tat-
putrāḥ pautrāḥ sa-bāndhavāḥ
tat-putra-pautra-naptṛṇāṁ
gotrāṇi ca na śṛṇmahe 6.3.16

“Your children, grandchildren, and other relatives are no longer on the earth. We do not hear anything of the descendents of your children, grandchildren, or great grandchildren. The dynasty no longer exists.”

tad gaccha sarva-mukhyāya
nara-ratnāya śāśvate
kanyā-ratnam idaṁ rājan
baladevāya dehi bhoḥ 6.3.17

“Therefore you should approach Lord Balarāma, who is the best of all, the jewel of men, and the person that lives eternally, and offer your jewel-daughter to Him.”

paripūrṇatamau sākṣād
golokādhipatī prabhū
bhuvo bhārāvatārāyā-
vatīrṇau bala-keśavau 6.3.18

“Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma are the two original Supreme Personalities of Godhead. They are Goloka’s two masters, who have descended to remove the earth’s burden.”

asaṅkhya-brahmāṇḍa-patī
vasudevātmajau harī
dvārakāyaṁ virājete
yadubhir bhakta-vatsalau 6.3.19

“Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, who are the two Supreme Lords, who are the two masters of numberless universes, who are Vasudeva’s two sons, and who are kind to Their devotees, are now spleniddly manifest in Dvārakā, with the Yādavas as Their companions.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
atha śrutvā vidhiṁ natvā
raivato nṛpa-sattamaḥ
āyayau dvārakāṁ bhūyaḥ
samṛddhaṁ taṁ samṛddhibhiḥ 6.3.20

Śrī Nārada said: “Hearing these words, the great king Raivata bowed down before Lord Brahmā and then quickly went to the supremely opulent city of Dvārakā.”

pāribarhe rathaṁ dattvā
viśvakarma-vinirmitam
sahasra-haya-samyuktaṁ
divya-yojana-vistṛtam 6.3.21

“As a wedding present he gave a chariot built by Viśvakarmā that was eight celestial miles long and yoked with a thousand horses, . . .”

divyāmbarāni ratnāni
brahma-dattāni maithila
datvāyayau tapas taptuṁ
badary-ākhyaṁ śubhāvaham 6.3.22

“. . . and he also gave many splendid garments and jewels he had received from Brahmā. After giving these gifts he left to perform austerities at sacred Badarikāśrama.”

tadā mahotsavaś cāsīd
yadu-puryāṁ gṛhe gṛhe
saṅkarṣaṇo ’tha bhagavān
revatyā virarāja ha 6.3.23

“Then there was a great festival in the capitol of the Yādavas. In home after home the Supreme Lord Balarāma appeared with Śrīmatī Revatī.”

Chapter 4 - Journey to the City of Kuṇḍina

śrī-nārada uvāca
atha śrī-kṛṣṇadevasya
vivāhaṁ śṛṇu maithila
sarva-pāpa-haraṁ puṇyaṁ
catur-varga-phala-pradam 6.4.1

Śrī Nārada said: “O king of Mithilā, please hear the story of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s wedding, a sacred story that removes all sins and grants the four goals of life.”

bhīṣmako nāma rājābhūd
vidarbheṣu pratāpavān
kuṇḍinādhipatiḥ śrīmān
sarva-dharma-vidāṁ varaḥ 6.4.2

“In the country of Vidarbha there was a powerful king named Bhīṣmaka, who ruled from his capitol Kuṇḍina. He was handsome and glorious and he was the best of the knowers of true religion.”

rukmiṇī tat-sutā jātā
śriyo mātrāti-sundarī
koṭi-candra-pratīkāśa
guṇa-bhūṣaṇa-bhūṣitā 6.4.3

“He had a daughter named Rukmiṇī, who was more beautiful than the goddess of fortune. She was decorated with all virtues and was more glorious than ten million moons.”

śrutvaikadā purā sā vai
man-mukhāc chrī-harer guṇān
paripūrṇatamaṁ taṁ vai
sa mene sādṛśaṁ patim 6.4.4

“From my mouth she once heard the transcendental virtues of Lord Kṛṣṇa. From that day she thought of attaining a husband like Him, the Supreme Personality of Godhead.”

tad-rūpaṁ sa-guṇaṁ śrutvā
man-mukhāt prīti-vardhanāt
sādṛśīṁ śrī-haris tāṁ vai
samudvoḍhuṁ mano dadhe 6.4.5

“From my mouth Lord Kṛṣṇa heard of her beauty and virtues. He fell in love with her and fixed his heart on marrying a girl like her.”

kṛṣṇa-bhāva-vidā rājñā
sarva-dharma-vidā bhṛśam
bhīṣmakenaiva kṛṣṇāya
dātuṁ tāṁ niścayaḥ kṛtaḥ 6.4.6

“Aware that Lord Kṛṣṇa had fallen in love with his daughter, King Bhīṣmaka, who knew all about true religion, decided to give his daughter to Him.”

yuva-rājas tato rukmī
taṁ nivārya prayatnataḥ
kṛṣṇa-śatruṁ mahā-vīraṁ
śiśupālam amanyata 6.4.7

“With a great effort prince Rukmī stopped him. Rukmī thought his sister should be given to Śiśupāla, a great warrior who was Kṛṣṇa’s enemy.”

tataḥ khinna-manāḥ bhaiṣmī
śrī-kṛṣṇāya mahātmane
dūtaṁ svaṁ preṣayām āsa
brāhmaṇaṁ mithileśvara 6.4.8

“Dejected, Rukmiṇī sent a brāhmaṇa messenger to noble-hearted Lord Kṛṣṇa.”

sa dvārakāṁ gato divyaṁ
śrī-kṛṣṇena prapūjitaḥ
bhuktavāṁs tatra cāsīno
viśrānto mandire hareḥ 6.4.9

“Arriving at glorious Dvārakā, the brāhmaṇa was worshiped by Lord Kṛṣṇa. The brāhmaṇa sat, ate, and rested in Lord Kṛṣṇa’s palace.”

pṛcchate kuśalaṁ sarvaṁ
śrī-kṛṣṇāya mahātmane
brāhmaṇas tad-anujñātas
tasmai sarvam avarṇayat 6.4.10

“First the brāhmaṇa inquired about noble-hearted Lord Kṛṣṇa’s welfare. Then, with the Lord’s permission, the brāhmaṇa recited Rukmiṇī’s letter to Him:”

svasti śrīkāra-pañcāḍhye
nityānanda-mahodadhau
śrīmad-divya-guṇaiḥ pūrṇe
koṭiśo natayo mama 6.4.11

“Greetings to You, the master of five opulences, a great ocean of eternal bliss, filled with splendid divine virtues. I bow down before You millions and millions of times.”

śam atrāstu ca tatrāstu
tatas tvat-patram āgatam
nāradoktena vacasā
jñāto ’si prakṛteḥ paraḥ 6.4.12

“Your letter brought happiness everywhere. From Nārada Muni’s words I can understand that You are beyond the world of matter.”

sarvaṁ jānāsi sarvajñas
tathā vakṣye vaco rahaḥ
vīra-bhāgaṁ tu māṁ viddhi
tvaṁ gṛhāṇa mahā-mate 6.4.13

“Although You know everything, I shall tell know something in private: Please know that I am a prize to be grasped by a hero like You. O noble-hearted one, please take me by force.”

mā caidyaḥ pratigṛhṇīyād
yathā siṁha-baliṁ mṛgaḥ
kathaṁ tvam udvahe durge
sthitam iti ca tac chṛṇu 6.4.14

“As a deer should not take the lion’s prize, so Śiśupāla should not take me. How can You kidnap me while I am in the fortress of this palace? Please listen.”

pūrve dyuḥ kula-devyās tu
yatrāsti mahatī hare
āgamiṣyāmy ahaṁ tatra
tatra māṁ tvaṁ gṛhāṇa bhoḥ 6.4.15

“One day before the wedding there will be a great festival for goddess Durgā, our family’s deity. O Kṛṣṇa, I will go to that festival and You can kidnap me then.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
rukmiṇyās tam abhiprāyaṁ
śrutvā brāhmaṇa-bhāṣitam
rathaḥ samyujyatām āśu
dārukaṁ prāha mānadaḥ 6.4.16

Śrī Nārada said: “When he heard the brāhmaṇa recite Rukmiṇī’s letter, noble Kṛṣṇa said to Dāruka, “Prepare a chariot at once.””

paścimāyāṁ tadā rātrau
vaikuṇṭha-prabhavaṁ param

hema-ratna-khacit-prabham
sad-aśvaiḥ śaibya-sugrīva-
meghapuṣpa-balāhakaiḥ
niyojitair dārukeṇa
cañcalaiś cāru-cāmaraiḥ
yuktaṁ mahā-rathaṁ divyaṁ
sahasrāditya-varcasam
āruhya sāratheḥ pṛṣṭhe
dhṛtvā śrī-pāda-paṅkajam
sva-hastena dvijaṁ tasmin
samāropya ramā-patiḥ
vidarbhān prayayau rājan
chrī-kṛṣṇo bhagavān hariḥ 6.4.17-20

“That night a chariot from Vaikuṇṭha appeared in the west. It was decorated with gold, jewels, and a network of tinkling ornaments and was splendid like a thousand suns. Dāruka yoked to it the restless cāmara-decorated horses Śaibya, Sugrīva, Meghapuṣpa, and Balāhaka, and then mounted the chariot behind them. Then Lord Kṛṣṇa, the goddess of fortune’s husband, placed His own lotus feet on the chariot, and with His own hand pulled the brāhmaṇa onto the chariot. O king, then the Lord went to the country of Vidarbha.”

kṛṣṇaṁ caikaṁ gataṁ hartuṁ
kanyāṁ tu nṛpa-maṇḍalāt
kali-praśaṅkito rāmaḥ
śrutvā bhrātṛ-sahāya-kṛt 6.4.21

“Hearing that Lord Kṛṣṇa had gone alone to kidnap Rukmiṇī from the circle of kings, and fearing a quarrel would follow, Lord Balarāma prepared to help His brother.”

nītvā yadu-balaṁ sarvaṁ
samartha-bala-vāhanam
vipakṣīyān nṛpān jetuṁ
balaḥ paścād yayau tvaram 6.4.22

“Taking with Him all the Yādavas’ powerful armies, Lord Balarāma went to defeat the enemy kings.”

kuṇḍinopavanaṁ prāptaḥ
sa-dvijaḥ sa-ratho hariḥ
santasthau tintiṇī-vrkṣe
āstīryāśva-paricchadam 6.4.23

“Arriving at the gardens outside Kuṇḍina, Kṛṣṇa and the brāhmaṇa stayed under a tintiṇī tree as Dāruka untied the horses.”

durāt sandṛśyate tasmāt
kuṇḍinaṁ tu puraṁ param
dīrgha-durga-samāyuktaṁ
sapta-yojana-vartulam 6.4.24

“In the distance they could see the great city of Kuṇḍina, which stood within a great fortress 56 miles around.”

durlaṅghyā durgamā yatra
parikhā jala-pūritā
dhanuḥ-śataṁ viṣṭrtāsti
cāturmasya-nadīva sā 6.4.25

“That fort was encircled by an impassable moat a hundred bows wide and overflowing with water like a river swollen by the monsoon.”

pañcāśad-dhasta-mānena
durga-bhittis tathordhva-gā
yatra ramyāṇi harmyāṇi
sphurad-dhema-śikhāni ca 6.4.26

“The city was surrounded by a wall fifty hands high. In the city were many beautiful palaces with glistening golden roofs.”

hema-kmbha-dhvaja-sphurjat-
tolakāni virejire
pārāvatā mauyūrāś ca
yatra tatra patanti ca 6.4.27

“There were many flags, glittering ornaments, and splendid golden spires. Many peacocks and pārāvatas flew here and there.”

śiśpuālāya svāṁ kanyāṁ
dāsyan rājā tu bhīṣmakaḥ
cakre vivāha-sambhāra-
sañcayaṁ ratna-maṇḍape 6.4.28

“About to offer his daughter to Siśupāla, King Bhīṣmaka made elaborate wedding preparations in a jewel pavilion.”

gīta-maṅgala-samyukte
narībhir bhavanottame
rarāja rukmiṇī rājan
siddhibhir bhūr yathā bhuvi 6.4.29

“As goddess Earth is splendidly manifested among many mystic powers in the earthly realm, so Rukmiṇī was splendidly manifested among many women in a palace filled with beautiful music.”

atharva-vid-dvijā bhaiṣmīṁ
su-snatāṁ ratna-vāsasām
cakrur mantrais tathā rakṣāṁ
baddhvā śāntiṁ vidhāya ca 6.4.30

“Then brāhmaṇas learned in the Atharva Veda approached the carefuully bathed and decorated with exquisite garments and precious jewels Rukmiṇī, chanted mantras for her protection and tied on her an amulet.”

haimānāṁ bhara-lakṣaṁ ca
muktānāṁ dvi-guṇaṁ tathā
sahasra-bhāraṁ vastrāṇāṁ
dhenūnām arbudāni ṣaṭ
gajāyutaṁ rathānāṁ ca
daśa-lakṣaṁ manoharam
daśa-koṭi-hayānāṁ ca
gudādi-tila-parvatān
sahasraṁ svarṇa-patrāṇāṁ
bhūṣaṇānāṁ tathāyutam
viprebhyaḥ pradadau rājā
bhīṣmako ’ti-mahā-manāḥ 6.4.31-33

“The noble-hearted King Bhīṣmaka gave in charity to the brāhmaṇas a hundred thousand bharas of gold, twice that much in pearls, a thousand bharas of valuable garments, six hundred million cows, a hundred million horses, many mountains of grains and molasses, a thousand golden cups, and ten thousand ornaments.”

tathā vai damaghoṣasya
śiśupālāya vai dvijāḥ
cakruḥ śāntiṁ paraṁ pūrvaṁ
rakṣā-bandhana-rūpiṇīm 6.4.34

“Then, on Damaghoṣa’s order, the brāhmaṇas gave a protective amulet to Śiśupāla.”

brāhmaṇair maṅgala-snānaṁ
pati-kañcuka-śobhitam
mukuṭopari vibhrājat-
puṣpa-mauli-dharaṁ śubham
hara-kaṅkana-keyūra-
śikhāmaṇi-vibhūṣitam

śiśupālaṁ vidhāya ca
āropya kariṇaṁ proccaṁ

“Placing his son, Śiśupāla, who had been ritually bathed by the brāhmaṇas, who was elegantly dressed as the bridegroom, who wore a crown decorated with splendid flowers, who wore necklaces, bracelets, armlets, and crest jewels, who was anointed with sweet fragrances, and who was greated with auspicious singing, instrumental music, and a shower of grains, on a great elephant, and then himself climbing on that elephant, King Damaghoṣa left his own city.”

jarāsandhena śālvena
dantavakreṇa dhīmatā
virūrathena pauṇḍreṇa
pāṛṣṇi-grāhena maithila
vikarṣan mahatīṁ senāṁ
damaghoṣo mahā-balaḥ
dundubhīn nādayān dīrghān
āyayau kuṇḍinaṁ puram 6.4.38

“Accompanied by Jarāsandha, Śalva, intelligent Dantavakra, and Vidūratha, and with Pauṇḍraka at the rear, powerful King Damaghoṣa, bringing with him a great army sounding kettledrums, went to Kuṇḍina City.”

sammukhād yadu-devasya
śrutvodyogaṁ nṛpāḥ pare
sahasraśaḥ samājagmuḥ
śiśupāla-sahāyinaḥ 6.4.40

“Hearing that Lord Kṛṣṇa had come, many thousands of other kings, all Śiśupāla’s allies, eagerly came.”

Chapter 5 - The Kidnapping of Śrī Rukmiṇī

śrī-nārada uvāca
dhyāyantī kṛṣṇa-pādābjaṁ
bhaiṣmī kamala-locanā
moghaṁ vā manute vārtāṁ
megha-śyāmam acintayat 6.5.1

Śrī Nārada said: “Although she thought her letter must have been fruitless, lotus-eyed Rukmiṇī continued to meditate on Lord Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet. Again and again she thought of Lord Kṛṣṇa, who is splendid as a dark monsoon cloud.”

śrī-rukmiṇy uvāca
aho tri-yāmāntarito vivāho
mamaiva nāgacchati kṛṣṇacandraḥ
na vedmi kiṁ kāraṇam atra dhātar
nāvartate ’dyāpi ca bhūmi-devaḥ 6.5.2

Śrī Rukmiṇī said: “My wedding will be in nine hours, and still Śrī Kṛṣṇacandra has not come. I don’t understand. O destiny, what is the reason for this delay? The brāhmaṇa-messenger I sent has not returned either.”

yadūttamo deva-varo mamaiṣa
dṛṣṭvā hi kiñcit kaluṣaṁ vidhātaḥ
kṛtodyamo nūnam atīva hasta-
grāhe na cāgacchati kiṁ karomi 6.5.3

“Perhaps Lord Kṛṣṇa, the master of the demigods and the best of the Yādavas, looks at me and sees that I am sinful and impure. Perhaps that is why He has not come to take my hand.”

hā durbhagāyāś ca na me vidhātā
na sānukūlaḥ kila candra-mauliḥ
na caika-danto vimukhā ca gaurī
gavo hi viprāś ca na sānūkulāḥ 6.5.4

“Brahmā is not kind to unfortunate me. Śiva is not kind. Gaṇeśa is not kind. Pārvatī is not kind. The cows and the brāhmaṇas are not kind to me.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
evaṁ vicintayantī sā
bhaiṣmī gehhāṭṭa-bhūmiṣu
paribhramantī śrī-kṛṣṇaṁ
paśyanti gṛha-śekharāt 6.5.5

Śrī Nārada said: “As she was thinking these thoughts and pacing back and forth in a room high in the palace, Rukmiṇī suddenly saw Śrī Kṛṣṇa below.”

tadaiva tasyā vāmāṅgaṁ
asphurat pratibhāṣaṇam
tena prasannā śrī-bhaiṣmī
kāla-jñā sarva-maṅgalā 6.5.6

“At that moment the left side of her body trembled. That trembling was the reply to her questions. Aware of the nature of this good omen, all-beautiful and auspicious Śrī Rukmiṇī was very pleased.”

kṛṣṇa-praṇodito vipraḥ
sadyaś cāgatavaṁs tadā
śrī-kṛṣṇāgamanaṁ tasyai
śanaiḥ sarvaṁ śaśaṁsa ha 6.5.7

“Sent by Lord Kṛṣṇa, the brāhmaṇa presented himself before her and told her that Lord Kṛṣṇa had come.”

tataḥ prasannā śrī-bhaiṣmī
tad-aṅghryoḥ praṇipatya sa
prāha tvad-vaṁśato vipra
na yāsyāmi vaco mama 6.5.8

“Very pleased, Śrī Rukmiṇī fell at the brāhmaṇa’s feet and said, “O brāhmaṇa, I will never leave your family. That is my promise.” Note: Śrī Rukmiṇī is Śrīmatī Lakṣmī-devī, the goddess of fortune. Here she promises that the brāhmaṇa’s family will never be in poverty.”

śrutvāgatau rāma-kṛṣṇau
vivāha-prekṣaṇotsukau
bhīṣmako nirgato netuṁ
brāhmaṇais tat-prabhāva-vit 6.5.9

“Hearing that Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, eager to see the wedding, had come, King Bhīṣmaka, aware of Their great power, went with many brāhmaṇas to greet Them.”

bhṛśaṁ maṅgala-pātreṣu
gandhākṣata-yuteṣu ca
vaso-ratna-cayaṁ dhṛtvā
gīta-vāditra-maṅgalaiḥ
koṭiśo madhu-parkānāṁ
kumbha-vyuhān vidhāya ca
pūjayitvātha vidhi-vad
rāma-kṛṣṇau pareśvarau 6.5.10-11

“Offering many millions of maṅgala-pātras, sweet fragrances, garments, jewels, and pots of madhu-parka, and accompanied by sweet singing and instrumental music, King Bhīṣmaka properly worshiped Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, the two Supreme Personalities of Godhead.”

aho cāsmai na datteyaṁ
iti khinna-manāḥ param
ānandena vane sthāpya
natvā sva-gṛham āyayau 6.5.12

“Unhappy at heart, he thought, “Alas, I did not offer her to Him!” Cheerfully giving Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma a place to stay in the garden, He bowed down before Them and then returned to his home.”

śrutvāgataṁ śrī-vasudeva-nandanaṁ
trailokya-lāvaṇya-nidhiṁ pareśvaram
āgatya netrāñjalibhiḥ puraukasaḥ
pāpuḥ paraṁ tan-mukha-paṅkajāmṛtam 6.5.13

“Hearing that Vasudeva’s son Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who is the Supreme Personality of Godhead and the most handsome person in the three worlds, had arrived, with the cupped hands of their eyes the people of Kuṇḍina City again and again drank the nectar of His lotus face.”

asyaiva bhāryā bhavituṁ hi rukmiṇī
yogyāsti nānye ’tyavadan puraukasaḥ
dattvā sva-puṇyāni vivāha-hetave
śrī-kṛṣṇa-lāvaṇya-kalā-nibandhakāḥ 6.5.14

“Enthralled by Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s handsomeness, the people of Kuṇḍina City said, “Rukmiṇī should marry Kṛṣṇa. No one be He should marry her.” The people of Kuṇḍika City donated the pious karmas of their previous births so that Kṛṣṇa could marry Rukmiṇī.”

kadāpi sākṣāc chvaśurasya mandiraṁ
samāgataṁ caivam aho vayaṁ janāḥ
drakṣyāma ārāt kṛta-kṛtyatāṁ tadā
vrajema loke bahu-jīvitena kim 6.5.15

“We will see Lord Kṛṣṇa when He goes to the palace of the bride’s father. Then our lives will become perfect. Why should we bother to spend many lives accumulating pious deeds?”

vadatsu lokeṣu ca bhīṣma-kanyakā-
dri-kanyakā-pūjana-hetave nṛpa
antaḥ-purāt sarva-sakhī-samanvitā
viniryayau kṛṣṇa-gṛhīta-mānasā 6.5.16

“O king, as the people were speaking these words, princess Rukmiṇī, her heart now captured by Lord Kṛṣṇa, left the palace with her friends to attend the worship of goddess Pārvatī.”

bherī-mṛdaṅgair bahu-dundubhi-svanaiḥ
su-gāyakair vandi-janaiś ca māgadhaiḥ
vārāṅganā-nṛtya-manojña-bhāvair
jayety abhūn maṅgala-śabda uccakaiḥ 6.5.17

“As the bherī, mṛdaṅga, and dundubhi drums sounded, the singers sang, the vandīs and māgadhas recited poetry, and the dancing girls gracefully danced, there were auspicious loud calls of “Victory!”

koṭīndu-bimba-dyutim ādadhānāṁ
bālārka-tāṭāṅka-dharāṁ śriyaṁ tām
sitātapatra-vyajanaiḥ sphuradbhiḥ
su-cāmaraiḥ pārśva-gaṇaḥ siṣeve 6.5.18

“Holding a white parasol and waving fans and cāmaras, many girls served Śrī Rukmiṇī, the goddess of fortune, who was splendid as ten million moons and who wore earrings splendid as the rising sun.”

kośād viniṣkṛṣya śitāsi-lakṣaṁ
padātayo vīra-janā itas tataḥ
tathāśva-gā vā rathino gajā-sthitāḥ
samudyatāstrā jugupur vidūrataḥ 6.5.19

“Taking a hundred thousand sharpened swords from the armory, footsoldiers stood here and there. Riding on horses, chariots, and elephants, other soldiers stood as guards with upraised weapons.”

devi-maṭhaṁ prāpya su-catvare sthitā
śāntā śucir dhauta-karāṅghri-paṅkajaḥ
gatvā samīpaṁ yata-vāk kṛtāñjalir
bheje bhavānīṁ bhava-bhīti-hāriṇīm 6.5.20

“Coming to goddess Pārvatī’s temple, peaceful and pure-hearted Rukmiṇī washed her lotus hands and feet and, observing a vow of silence, entered the courtyard. Approaching the deity of the goddess, who removes the fear of repeated birth and death, Rukmiṇī worshiped her with folded hands.”

durge sva-saṁsthāna-yute śive śubhe
nāmāmi tubhyaṁ satataṁ bhavāni te
bhūyāt patir me bhagavān pareśvaraḥ
śrī-kṛṣṇacandraḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ svayam 6.5.21

She said: “O Durgā, O wife of Lord Śiva, O beautiful and glorious one, I offer my respectful obeissances to you again and again. I pray that Śrī Kṛṣṇacandra, the original Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is above the world of matter, will become my husband.”

evaṁ śubhe mā vada kṛṣṇa-nāma
caidyaṁ samuddiśya varaṁ gṛhāṇa
itthaṁ vadantīṣu sakhīṣu bhaiṣmī
bhūyo bhavānī-bhavane jagāda 6.5.22

“When her friends said, “O beautiful one, don’t say the word Kṛṣṇa. You should pray that Śiśupāla will become your husband,” Rukmiṇī spoke in the following words in the temple of goddess Pārvatī:”

ajānatīyaṁ tava cāmba bālā
tathā vadantīṣu sakhīṣu bhaiṣmī
gandhākṣatair dhūpa-vibhūṣaṇādyaiḥ
sraṅ-mālya-dīpāvalī-bhoga-vastraiḥ
apūpa-tāmbūla-phalekṣubhiś ca
bheje bhavānīṁ parayā ca bhaktyā
natvātha tāṁ vā bahu-bhūṣaṇādyaiḥ
sampūjya saubhāgyavatīr nanāma 6.5.23-24

“O Mother Pārvatī, my friends that say this are only little girls. They do not understand the truth about you.” Then with great devotion Rukmiṇī worshiped goddess Pārvatī by offering her sweet fragrances, unbroken grains, incense, ornaments, garlands, necklaces, lamps, delicious foods, garments, cakes, betelnuts, fruits, sugarcane, and many other gifts. Then Rukmiṇī bowed down before the fortunate brāhmaṇa women and worshiped them with many ornaments and other gifts.”

sarvāḥ striyas tāḥ pradadur varāṇi
su-maṅgalāśir-vacanāni tasyai
rūpaṁ sadā te śatarūpayā samaṁ
śīlaṁ sadā śailasutā-samaṁ prabhau 6.5.25

“All the brāhmaṇa women reciprocated by giving gifts to Rukmiṇī. They also blessed her by saying, “May you be beautiful like Śatarūpā. May you be virtuous like goddess Pārvatī.”

Chapter 6 - The Yādavas’ Victory When Śrī Rukmiṇī is Kidnapped

itthaṁ vipra-vadhūnāṁ sad-
āśīrbhir abhinanditā
devīṁ punar vipra-vadhūḥ
praṇanāma muhur muhuḥ 6.6.1

“Blessed in this way by the brāhmaṇa women, Rukmiṇī again and again bowed down before them and before goddess Pārvatī.”

tyaktvā muni-vrataṁ bhaiṣmī
girijā-gṛhatas tataḥ
sahalībhiḥ sakhībhiś ca
niścakrāma śanaiḥ śanaiḥ 6.6.2

“Now abandoning her vow of silence, in the company of her friends Rukmiṇī left Pārvatī’s temple, walking with exaggerated slowness.”

koṭi-candra-pratīkāśāṁ
bhaiṣmīṁ kamala-locanām
akasmād dadṛśur vīrāḥ
su-nidhiṁ nirdhanā yathā 6.6.3

“As poverty-stricken beggars stare at a priceless treasure, so the host of soldiers stared at lotus-eyed Rukmiṇī, who was glorious as millions of moons.”

āśvārūhaś ca rathino
gajinaś ca padātayaḥ
samāgatā rakṣiṇas te
mumuhur vīkṣya rukmiṇīm 6.6.4

“Riding on horses, chariots, and elephants, and some walking on foot, many guards came, stared at Rukmiṇī, and became bewildered with desire.”

tad-apaṅga-smitais tīkṣṇair
bāṇaiḥ kāma-dhanuś-cyutaiḥ
ujjhitāstrā nipetuḥ kāv
arditāḥ sainikās tadā 6.6.5

“Wounded by the sharp Kāma’s arrows of her smiling eyes, the soldiers dropped their weapons and fell to the gound.”

rathena vāyu-vegena
ghaṇṭā-mañjīra-nādinā
naiḥśreya-sambhavair aśvair
yutenāti-patākinā
śīghraṁ sva-sainya-saṅghaṭṭāt
tat-sainyaṁ samvidārayāt
vāyur yathā padma-vanaṁ
harir dāruka-sārathiḥ
strī-kadambakam etyāśu
paśyatāṁ dviṣatāṁ prabhuḥ
samāropya rathaṁ bhaiṣmīṁ
tārkṣya-putraḥ sudhām iva 6.6.6-8

“Riding on a chariot fast as the wind, a chariot decorated with a glorious flag and tinkling bells and ornaments, pulled by the best of horses, and driven by Dāruka, Lord Kṛṣṇa left His army, cut apart the opposing army, and raced to the cluster of women as a wind enters a lotus forest. As His enemies looked on, He placed Rukmiṇī on His chariot as Garuḍa would gather up a flood of nectar.”

devānāṁ paśyatāṁ rājan
rāja-kanyāṁ jahāra ha
divyaṁ śastrottamaṁ śārṅgaṁ
dhanuṣ ṭaṅkārayan muhuḥ 6.6.9

“O king, again and again Lord Kṛṣṇa twanged His Śārṅga bow, the best of weapons, and, as the demigods looked on, kidnapped beautiful Princess Rukmiṇī.”

tato vegena mahatā
sva-sainyaṁ cāgate harau
deva-dundubhayo nedur
yadu-dundubhayas tadā 6.6.10

“When Kṛṣṇa quickly returned to His own army, the demigods and the Yādavas sounded drums of victory.”

siddhāś ca siddha-kanyāś ca
śrī-kṛṣṇasya rathopari
harṣitā vavṛṣur devāḥ
puṣpair nandana-sambhavaiḥ 6.6.11

“Filled with happiness, the demigods, siddhas, and the siddhas’ young daughters showered on Lord Kṛṣṇa’s chariot flowers from the Nandana gardens.”

tato yayau jayārāvaiḥ
śanai rāma-yuto hariḥ

babhūva tumulaṁ yuddhaṁ

rathino rathibhis tatra

astrāndhakāre sañjāte

baladevānujo vīro

gada-bāṇa-vibhinnāṅgā
rathi-niśchinna-kañcukāḥ

padātayaś chinna-padā
gadā-bāṇāgata-vyathāḥ

aśvārūḍhāḥ ke ’pi vīrāḥ

petū raṇāṅgane sāśvā

“Struck by Gada’s arrows, many cavalrymen fell to the battleground like bṛhatī fruits.”

gada-bāṇair bhinna-kumbhā

parāṁ vyathāṁ gato yuddhe

lakṣa-bhāra-mayī gurvī
dṛḍhā kaumodakī yathā

vajreṇendro yathā girim 6.6.33

“With that club, heavy as a hundred thousand bhāras and strong as Kṛṣṇa’s club Kaumodakī, Gada struck Śālva as with his thunderbolt Indra strikes a mountain.”

gadā-prahāra-mathito

“Struck by the club, Śālva fell to the ground. Then Pauṇḍraka, Jarāsandha, Dantavakra, and Vidūratha angrily surrounded Gada. “Then, picking up His own club, Lord Balarāma hurried there and struck Dantavakra in his gruesome mouth.”

tato musala-ghāṭena

mukhe cakre ’pi yo dantaḥ

“With that blow from Lord Balarāma’s club, a tooth suddenly fell from the warrior Dantavakra’s mouth.”

“Then, as Rukmiṇī and the demons’ enemy Kṛṣṇa laughed, with His club angry Balarāma struck Pauṇḍraka, Jarāsandha, and Vidūratha, making them fall unconscious, bathed in their own blood.”

senāṁ samāgatāṁ sarvāṁ

musalenāhanat kruddho
baladevo mahā-balaḥ 6.6.42

“When the demons’ army came, angry Lord Balarāma dragged it with His plow and pounded it with His club.”

daśa-yojana-paryantaṁ

“Jarāsandha and the other kings that had somehow escaped death fled the battle, ran to Śiśupāla, whose wedding festival was now completely in ruins, and said to him:”

Chapter 7 - The Marriage of Śrī Rukmiṇī

śrī-nārada uvāca
rukmiṇyā haraṇaṁ śrutvā

pratijñām akarod rukmī
śṛṇvataṁ sarva-bhubhujām 6.7.1

Śrī Nārada said: “Hearing that Rukmiṇī was kidnapped and his friends were all defeated, Rukmī, as all the kings listened, spoke the following vow:”

ahatvā samare kṛṣṇaṁ
apratyūha ca rukmiṇīm
kuṇḍinaṁ na pravekṣyāmi
satyam etad bravīmi vaḥ 6.7.2

“If I do not kill Kṛṣṇa and return with Rukmiṇī, then I will never again enter the city of Kuṇḍina. I speak to you the truth.”

ity uktvā kavacaṁ divyaṁ
ghanam arbuda-nirmitam
śiras-trāṇaṁ sindhujaṁ ca
sa dadhāra mahodbhaṭaḥ 6.7.3

“After speaking these words, the great warrior Rukmī dressed himself in thick armor made on Mount Arbuda and a great helmet made by the ocean’s shore.”

sauvīrasya dhanuḥ śāli-

“Then he took a bow made in the country of Sauvīra, two quivers made in the country of Śālilāṭa, a bow made in the country of the mlecchas, a shield made in the country of Kauṭa, . . .”

baddha-godhāṅguli-trāṇaḥ
kirīṭī ratna-kuṇḍalaḥ
rukmāṅgadas tadā rukmī
yuddhaṁ kartuṁ mano dadhe 6.7.6

jaitraṁ rathaṁ samāruhya

tvaraṁ muñca svasāraṁ me

nidhāya śīghraṁ kodaṇḍaṁ

santatāḍa mṛdhe hariḥ 6.7.16

mahā-śaktiṁ sphurat-prabham

praharad dharaye so ’pi

“When Rukmī threw a club at Him, Lord Kṛṣṇa used His cakra to break into pieces.”

santāḍito ’pi bhagavān

taṁ jaghāna raṇāṅgane 6.7.22

kiñcid-vyākula-mānasaḥ

jagrāha khaḍga-carmaṇī 6.7.23

“Hit by the parigha, Rukmī became agitated at heart. Hurling insults at Lord Kṛṣṇa, he took up his sword and shield.”

tat-khaḍgaṁ carmaṇā chittvā

“Seeing Rukmī standing, without sword or gloves, before Him, Lord Kṛṣṇa grabbed him with both arms, threw him to the ground, and pounced on him as a lion pounces on a deer. Then the Lord angrily drew His sharp sword named Nandaka.”

dṛṣṭvā bhrātṛ-vadhodyuktaṁ
rukmiṇī bhaya-vihvalā
patitvā pādayor bhārtur
uvāca karuṇaṁ satī 6.7.27

“Seeing that her brother was on the verge of death, saintly terrified saintly Rukmiṇī fell at her husband’s feet and spoke very piteous words.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
paritrāsair vilapatīṁ
duḥkha-śuṣyan-mukhīṁ priyām
ruddha-kaṇṭhīṁ satīṁ vīkṣya
nyavartata hariḥ svayaṁ 6.7.29

Śrī Nārada said: “Seeing that saintly Rukmiṇī was frightened and weeping, her mouth dry with suffering, Lord Kṛṣṇa stopped Himself.”

baddhvā taṁ kaṭi-bandhena
khaḍgena śita-dhāriṇā
vapanaṁ smaśru-keśānāṁ
cakārārdha-mukhe hariḥ 6.7.30

“With a sash tying him up, with His sharp sword Lord Kṛṣṇa shaved the hair and beard of half of Rukmī’s face.”

akṣauhiṇī-dvayaṁ jitvā
rāmaḥ prāptaḥ sa-sainikaḥ
baddhaṁ virūpiṇaṁ dīnaṁ
rukmiṇaṁ tu dadarśa ha 6.7.31

“After defeating the two akṣauhiṇīs, Lord Balarāma and His army returned. Then Balarāma saw poor Rukmī tied up and made very funny-looking.”

vimucya baddhaṁ sa-dayaḥ
prāha nirbhartsayan harim
asādhv idaṁ tvayā kṛṣṇa
kṛtaṁ loka-jugupsitam 6.7.32

“Untying him, compassionate Balarāma rebuked Kṛṣṇa, saying, “Kṛṣṇa, how horrible is this thing You did! It is not right.”

hāsyaṁ vaiśāli-bhadrāṇāṁ
na hi caitādṛśaṁ bhavet
yasyāḥ sahodare mukhye
virūpe ca tvayā kṛte 6.7.33

“You should not have made Rukmiṇī’s brother’s face so funny-looking that everyone will laugh at him.”

kiṁ vadiṣyati sāpi tvāṁ
bhrātur vairūpya-cintayā
mā śokaṁ kuru kalyāṇi
svasthā bhava śuci-smite 6.7.34

“What will Rukmiṇī say to You?” Then Lord Balarāma said to Rukmiṇī, “O beautiful one, don’t be unhappy that your brother has been made so ugly-looking. O girl with the beautiful smile, please be happy.”

ārya-putri mahā-buddhe
mā śokaṁ kuru durmanāḥ
sarvaṁ kāla-kṛtaṁ manye
priyam apriyam eva vā 6.7.35

“O noble girl, O intelligent one, don’t be unhappy. I think it is time that brings everything, whether pleasant or unpleasant.”

vāyor ghanāvalir iva
vaśe yasyākhilaṁ jagat
taṁ kālam īśvaraṁ viddhi
viṣṇuṁ kalayatāṁ prabhum 6.7.36

“As clouds are under the control of the wind, so this entire universe is under the control of time. Please know that time is really Lord Viṣṇu, the supreme controller, the master of all masters.”

ahaṁ mameti bhāvo ’yaṁ
jagato bandha-kāraṇam
tābhyāṁ virahito bhāvo
mokṣa eva na saṁśayaḥ 6.7.37

“The thoughts, ’It is I’ and ’It is mine’ bind one to the material world. When one becomes free from these material thoughts of pride and possessiveness he attains liberation. Of this there is no doubt.”

sukha-duḥkha-prado nānyaḥ
puruṣasyātma-vibhramaḥ
mitrodāsīna-ripavaḥ
saṁsāra-tamasā kṛtāḥ 6.7.38

“Lord Viṣṇu alone is the giver of happiness and distress. No one else gives them. The thought that someone is a friend, someone else is a neutral party, and someone else is an enemy is an illusion created by the material nature.”

evaṁ rāmeṇa devena
bodhito bhīṣmakātmajaḥ
vaimanasyaṁ parityajya
rukmiṇī ca yayau mudam 6.7.39

“Educated by Lord Balarāma’s words, Rukmī and Rukmiṇī discarded their unhappiness and became happy.”

rukmī tu tābhyām utsṛṣṭo
vitathātma-manorathaḥ
smaran virūpa-kāraṇaṁ
tapase sa mano dadhāt 6.7.40

“In this way Rukmī’s desires were thwarted by Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma. Remembering how he had been made so ugly, Rukmī fixed his mind on performing austerities.”

vārito mantri-mukhyaiś ca
kuṇḍinaṁ na gataḥ punaḥ
cakre bhojakaṭaṁ nāma
nivāsāya puraṁ param 6.7.41

“Forbidden by his advisors, he did not return to Kuṇḍina. Instead he went to the city of Bhojakaṭa, and there he lived.”

rukmiṇyā saha govindaḥ
sa-rāmo yadubhir vṛtaḥ
dvārakāṁ prayayau rājan
nādayan jaya-dundubhīn 6.7.42

“O king, accompanied by Rukmiṇī, Balarāma, and the Yādavas, Lord Kṛṣṇa returned to Dvārakā, where the drums of victory sounded.”

jāte mahotsave puryāṁ
rukmiṇīṁ rucirānanām
upayeme vidhānena
mārgaśīrṣe hariḥ svayam 6.7.43

“In the month of Mārgasīrṣa (November-December) there was a great festival in Dvārakā City as Lord Kṛṣṇa married beautiful-faced Rukmiṇī.”

harer vivāhe sati rukmiī-pateḥ
śrī-rukmiṇī bhūṣita-rukma-mandirā
purandarasyāpi yathāmaravatī
dvārāvatī puṇyavatī tathā babhau 6.7.44

“On the day of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s marriage to Rukmiṇī, the sacred city of Dvārakā, which is decoraqted with many golden palaces, was glorious like King Indra’s city of Amarāvatī.”

Chapter 8 - The Marriages of All the Queens

anyāsāṁ kṛṣṇa-patnīnāṁ
maṅgalaṁ śṛṇu maithila
sarva-pāpa-haraṁ puṇyaṁ
āyur-vardhanam uttamam 6.8.1

“O king of Mithilā, please hear the auspicious story of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s other queens, a sacred story that removes all sins and increases the span of one’s life.”

satrājitāya sūryeṇa
dattaḥ sākṣāt syamantakaḥ
ugrasenāya sa maṇiḥ
śrī-kṛṣṇenābhiyācitaḥ 6.8.2

“When the sun-god gave the Syamantaka jewel to Satrājit, Lord Kṛṣṇa asked that the jewel be given to King Ugrasena.”

satrājitas taṁ na dadau
dravya-lobhena maithila
dine dine svarṇa-bhārān
aṣṭau yaḥ sṛjati svataḥ 6.8.3

“O king of Mithilā, out of greed King Satrājit did not give away the jewel, which daily created eight bhāras of gold.”

atha prasenas tad-bhrātā
maṇiṁ kaṇṭhe nidhāya saḥ
saindhavaṁ hayam āruhya
mṛgayāṁ vyacarad vane 6.8.4

“Then, placing the jewel on his neck, Satrājit’s brother Prasena mounted a sindhu horse and went hunting in the forest.”

siṁhena māritaḥ so ’pi
siṁho jāmbavatā hataḥ
gṛhītvā taṁ maṇiṁ sadyo
jāmbavān sva-guhāṁ gataḥ 6.8.5

“A lion killed Prasena. Then Jāmbavān killed the lion and took the jewel to his cave.”

kṛṣṇena nihato bhrātā
maṇigrīvo vanaṁ gataḥ
nāyātaḥ sva-sabhā-madhye
iti satrājito ’bravīt 6.8.6

Satrājit said: “When my brother, wearing the jewel around his neck, went to the forest, Kṛṣṇa killed him. That is why my brother has not returned to his people.”

bhagavān duryaśo-lipto
nāgarais tu vanaṁ gataḥ
prasenam aśvaṁ siṁhaṁ ca
hataṁ prekṣya mahā-mate 6.8.7

“O noble-hearted one, Lord Kṛṣṇa, His good reputation tainted by the rumor, went with some people of the city to the forest. There He saw Prasena, the horse, and the lion, all of them dead.”

rkṣa-rāja-bilaṁ gatvā
maṇiṁ hartuṁ svayaṁ hariḥ
yuddhaṁ kṛtvāṣṭa-viṁśāhaṁ
ajayad rkṣa-nāyakam 6.8.8

“Entering Jāmbavān’s cave to recover the jewel, for 28 days Lord Kṛṣṇa fought with Jāmbavān, the king of the ṛkṣas.”

tena dattā jāmbavatī
haraye kanyakā śubhā
maṇinā saha rājendra
dvārakām āyayau hariḥ 6.8.9

“O king of kings, Jāmbavān gave the jewel and his beautiful daughter, Jāmbavatī, to Lord Kṛṣṇa. Then the Lord returned to Dvārakā.”

satrājitāya pradadau
maṇiṁ nirlāñchanaḥ prabhuḥ
vrīḍito ’vāṅ-mukho bhīto
rājā satrājito maṇim
gṛhītvāpi punas tasmai
śrī-kṛṣṇāya mahātmane
satyabhāmāṁ sutāṁ prādāc
chānty-arthaṁ maithileśvara 6.8.10-11

“His good reputation restored, Lord Kṛṣṇa gave the Jewel to Satrājit. Embarrassed, frightened, and speechless, Satrājit gave his daughter, Satyabhāmā, in marriage to Lord Kṛṣṇa to pacify Him.”

pāṇḍavānāṁ sahāyārthaṁ
indraprasthaṁ gato hariḥ
tatraiva varṣikān māsān
nyavātsīd bandhu-vatsalaḥ 6.8.12

“Then Lord Kṛṣṇa, who dearly loves His relatives, went to Hastināpura and stayed there for the months of the monsoon to help the Pāṇḍavas.”

ekadā ratham āruhya
harir gāṇḍīvinā saha
su-nire yamunā-tīre
mṛgayārthī viniryayau 6.8.13

“One day Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna went hunting on the shore of the beautiful Yamunā.”

tapaś carantī kālindī
śrī-kṛṣṇaṁ varam icchati
darśitā pāṇḍavenāpi
tāṁ gṛhītvā jagāma ha 6.8.14

“There Arjuna found Kālindī, who had been performing austerities to attain Lord Kṛṣṇa as her husband. Arjuna showed her to Lord Kṛṣṇa, and He accepted her and took her with Him.”

dvārakām etya kālindīṁ
sūrya-kanyāṁ manoharām
upayeme vidhānena
vitanvan maṅgalaṁ param 6.8.15

“Returning to Dvārakā, Lord Kṛṣṇa married beautiful Kālindī, the sun-god’s daughter, in a proper and auspicious ritual.”

āvantya-rāja-tanujāṁ
mitravindāṁ manoharām
svayamvare tāṁ jahāra
bhagavān rukmiṇīṁ yathā 6.8.16

“Then, as He had kidnapped Rukmiṇī, Lord Kṛṣṇa kidnapped beautiful Mitravindā, the princess of Avantya-deśa, at the time of her svayamvara.”

nagnajit-kanyakāṁ satyāṁ
damitvā sapta go-vṛṣān
paśyatāṁ sarva-lokānāṁ
upayeme hariḥ svayam 6.8.17

“Then, after subduing seven bulls as all the people watched, Lord Kṛṣṇa married Satyā, the daughter of KIng Nagnajit.”

kaikeya-rāja-tanujāṁ
bhadrāṁ tu bhagavān hariḥ
kālindīm iva tāṁ śaśvad
upayeme vidhānataḥ 6.8.18

“Then, as He had married Kālindī, Lord Kṛṣṇa married Bhadrā, the princess of Kaikeya, in a proper ritual.”

bṛhatsena-sutāṁ rājal
lakṣmaṇāṁ lakṣaṇair yutām
chittvā matsyam arīn jitvā
jagrāha bhagavān hariḥ 6.8.19

“O king, after piercing a fish and defeating many rivals, Lord Kṛṣṇa took virtuous Lakṣmaṇā, the daughter of King Bṛhatsena.”

tathā ṣoḍaśa-sāhasraṁ
śataṁ ca nṛpa-kanyakāḥ
bhaumaṁ hatvā tan-nirodhād
āhṛtāś cāru-darśanāḥ 6.8.20

“Then Lord Kṛṣṇa killed the demon Bhaumāsura and released from his prison 16,100 beautiful princesses.”

tāsāṁ muhūrta ekasmin
nānāgāreṣu yoṣitām
sa-vidhiṁ jagṛhe pāṇīn
nānā-rūpaḥ sva-māyayā 6.8.21

“By the arrangement of His yogamāyā potency expanding into many forms, Lord Kṛṣṇa accepted the hands of all these girls in many different rooms in a single moment.”

ekaikaśas tāḥ kṛṣṇasya
putrān daśa daśābalāḥ
ajīnanann anavamān
pituḥ sarvātma-sampadā 6.8.22

“Each wife gave birth to ten exalted sons, each son having all his father’s virtues.”

rukmiṇyāṁ bhīṣma-kanyāyāṁ
pradyumnaḥ prathamo ’bhavat
kāmadevāvatāro ’yaṁ
pitṛvat-sarva-lakṣaṇaḥ 6.8.23

“From Rukmiṇī was born the first son, Pradyumna, who had all the transcendental qualities of His father, and who was an incarnation of Kāmadeva.”

śambaro nirdayas tokaṁ
hṛtvābdhau taṁ samākṣipat
matsyodare gato so ’pi
na mamāra hareḥ sutaḥ 6.8.24

“Then merciless Śambara kidnapped the infant Pradyumna and threw him in the ocean. Lord Kṛṣṇa’s son Pradyumna entered the belly of a great fish and did not die.”

matsyodarān nirgato ’sau
bhāryayā paripālitaḥ
jñātvā śatru-kṛtāṁ vārtāṁ
sa kaṛṣṇī rūḍha-yauvanaḥ 6.8.25

“Taken from the fish’s belly, and carefully protected by his wife, Pradyumna suddenly grew to become a young man. Then he understood what his enemy had done.”

hatvā taṁ śambaraṁ śatruṁ
bhāryayā varayā yutaḥ
dvārakām āyayau rājaṁs
citraṁ karma ca tasya tat 6.8.26

“O king, after killing his enemy Śambara, Pradyumna went with his beautiful wife to Dvārakā. His deeds are certainly very wonderful.”

sa rukmiṇo duhitaraṁ
hṛtvā bhojakaṭāt purāt
svayamvara-sthalād rājann
upayeme mahā-rathaḥ 6.8.27

“The great chariot-fighter Pradyumna also kidnapped Rukmī’s daughter from the place of her svayamvara in Bhojakaṭa City and married her.”

tasmāt suto ’niruddho ’bhūn
nāgāyuta-balānvitaḥ
sura-jyeṣṭhāvatāro ’yaṁ
śāradendīvara-prabhaḥ 6.8.28

“From Pradyumna was born a son, Aniruddha, who was strong as ten thousand elephants and glorious as a blue autumn lotus. He was an incarnation of the first of the demigods.”

catur-vyūhāvatārasya
paripūrṇatamasya hi
evaṁ vicitraṁ caritaṁ
vivāhānāṁ su-maṅgalam 6.8.29

“This is the wonderful and auspicious story of the marriages of Lord Kṛṣṇa, the origin of the catur-vyūha expansions of the Lord.”

Chapter 9 - The Arrival of Śrī Dvārakā

śrī-bahulāśva uvāca
triśu lokeśu vikhyātā
dhanyā vai dvārakā-purī
paripūrṇatamaḥ sākśāc
chrī-kṛṣṇo yatra vāsa-kṛt 6.9.1

Śrī Bahulāśva said: “Glorious Dvārakā City is famous in the three worlds. Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the original Supreme Personality of Godhead, resides there.”

śrī-kṛśṇasyāṣga-sambhūtā
purī dvāravatī śrutā
kasmād ihāgatā brahman
kasmin kāle vada prabho 6.9.2

“I have heard that Dvārakā City was manifested from Lord Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental body. How and when did it come to this earth? O master, please tell.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
sādhu sādhu tvayā pṛśṭaṁ
dvārakāgama-kāraṇam
yac chrutvā śuddhatāṁ yāti
loka-ghāty api pātakī 6.9.3

Śrī Nārada said: “Excellent. You have asked an excellent question. By hearing the reason for Dvārakā’s arrival on this earth, even a great sinner, or even a murderer becomes pure at heart.”

śaryātir nāma rājābhūc
cakravartī manoḥ sutaḥ
cakāra rājyaṁ dharmeṇa
varśānām ayutaṁ bhuvi 6.9.4

“There was a king named Śaryāti who for ten thousand years ruled the earth according to religious principles.”

uttānabarhir ānarto
bhūriśeṇa iti trayaḥ
śaryāter abhavan putrāḥ
sarva-dharma-bhṛtāṁ varāḥ 6.9.5

“Śaryāti had three very pious sons sons: Uttānabarhi, Anarta, and Bhūriśeṇa.”

uttānabarhiśe purvāṁ
bhūriśeṇāya dakśiṇām
paścimāṁ ca diśaṁ sarvāṁ
ānartāya dadau nṛpaḥ 6.9.6

“To Uttānabarhi King Śaryāti gave the eastern part of his kingdom, to Bhūriśeṇa he gave the southern part, and to Anarta he gave the western part.”

mameyaṁ hi mahī kṛtsnā
mayā dharmeṇa pālitā
balārjitā baliśṭhena
yūyaṁ taṁ pālayiśyatha 6.9.7

“King Śaryāti said to them: This entire earth is mine. I rule and protect it according to religious principles. I am most powerful. I have conquered this earth by my own power. In the future you three will also rule and protect this earth.”

pitur vacaḥ samākarṇya
ānarto madhyamaḥ sutaḥ
jñānī jñānamayaṁ vākyaṁ
uvāca prahasann iva 6.9.8

“Hearing his father’s words, the middle son, Anarta, who was very wise, laughed and spoke words of great wisdom.”

śrī-ānarta uvāca
taveyaṁ na mahī kṛtsnā
na tvayā pālitā kvacit
na tvad-balārjitā rājan
baliśṭho bhagavān vibhuḥ 6.9.9

Śrī Anarta said: “You do not own this earth. Nor do you rule and protect it. Nor did you conquer it with your own power. Only the Supreme Personality of Godhead is the all-powerful ruler of this earth.”

mahī śrī-kṛśṇadevaṣya
tenaiva paripālitā
tat-tejasā jitā kṛtsnā
baliśṭho na hareḥ samaḥ 6.9.10

“The earth is Lord Kṛṣṇa’s property. He rules and protects it. He conquered it with His great power. No one is powerful like Lord Kṛṣṇa.”

sa eva viśvaṁ sva-kṛtaṁ
sṛjaty atti ca pāti ca
sa eva brahma paramaṁ
kālaḥ kalayatāṁ prabhuḥ 6.9.11

“He creates, maintains, and destroys this universe. He is the Supreme Brahman. He is time, the master of all that subdue.”

yo ’ntaḥ praviśya bhūtāni
bhūtair apy akhilāśrayaḥ
sa viśvākhyo ’dhiyajño ’sau
paripūrṇatamaḥ svayam 6.9.12

“Himself. He has entered within all living entities and all elements. He is the shelter and resting place of all living entities and all elements. He is the entire universe. He is the object of all sacrifices. He is the most perfect Supreme Personality of Godhead.”

yad-bhayād vati vāto ’yaṁ
sūryas tapati yad-bhayāt
yad-bhayād varśate devo
mṛtyuś carati yad-bhayāt 6.9.13

“Out of fear of Him the wind blows. Out of fear of Him the sun shines. Out of fear of Him Indra gives rain. Out of fear of Him death moves on the earth.”

paripūrṇatamaṁ sākśāc
chrī-kṛśṣaṁ parameśvaram
bhaja sarvātmanā rājann
ahaṇkāra-vivarjitaḥ 6.9.14

“free from false ego. O king, please become free from pride and false ego and with all your heart worship Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
jñānaṁ prāpto ’pi śaryātir
ākśiptaḥ putra-vāk-śaraiḥ
ānartaṁ sva-sutaṁ prāha
ruśā prasphuritādharaḥ 6.9.15

“Wounded by the arrows of his son’s words, and his lips trembling in anger even though what he was confronted with was the truth, Śaryāti spoke to his son Anarta.”

śaryātir uvāca
dūraṁ gaccha asad-buddhe
guruvad bhāśase katham
yāvad bhūtaṁ tu me rājyaṁ
tāvat tvaṁ me mahīṁ vasa 6.9.16

Śaryāti said: “Fool, get out! Why do you talk as if you were my teacher? You may not live any place in my kingdom.”

yas tvayārādhitaḥ kṛśṣaḥ
so ’pi sarva-sahāya-kṛt
na navīnāṁ kiṁ mahīṁ te
bhagavān eva dāsyati 6.9.17

“Take help from this Kṛṣṇa you worship. Will He not give you some new land not now a part of the earth?”

śrī-nārada uvāca
ity uktas tu tadānārto
rājānaṁ prāha mānadaḥ
yatra te ca mahī-rājyaṁ
tatra vāso na me bhavet 6.9.18

Śrī Nārada said: “Addressed in this way, respectful Anarta said to the king, “I will not live anywhere in your kingdom, which extends to all the countries of the earth.””

pitrā niḥsārito rājñāpy
ānarto ’bdhi-taṭaṁ gataḥ
velām etya tapas tepe
varśānām ayutaṁ jale 6.9.19

“Banished by his father, Anarta went to the seacoast and entered the ocean, where he performed austerities for ten thousand years.”

prema-lakśaṇayā bhaktyā
santuśṭo bhagavān hariḥ
tasmai svaṁ darśanaṁ dattvā
varaṁ brūhīty uvāca ha 6.9.20

“Pleased by his loving devotion, Lord Kṛṣṇa appeared before him and said, “Ask for a boon.”

kṛtāñjali-puṭo bhūtvā-
narta utthāya śīghrataḥ
nanāma kṛṣṇa-pādābjaṁ
romāñcī prema-vihvalaḥ 6.9.21

“Overcome with love, and the hairs of his body erect with joy, Anarta quickly stood up and with folded hands bowed before Lord Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet.”

śrī-ānarta uvāca
namas te vāsudevāya
namaḥ saṇkarśaṇāya ca
pradyumnāyāniruddhāya
sātvatāṁ pataye namaḥ 6.9.22

Śrī Anarta said: “I offer my respectful obeisances to You, who are Lord Vāsudeva. I offer my respectful obeisances to You, who are Lord Saṇkarśaṇa, Lord Pradyumna, and Lord Aniruddha. I offer my respectful obeisances to You, who are the master of the devotees.”

pitrā niśkāsito deva
tvām ahaṁ śaraṇaṁ gataḥ
dehi mahyaṁ bhūmim anyāṁ
yatra vāso hi me bhavet 6.9.23

“I, who was banished by my father, now take shelter of You. O Lord, please give me a country where I may reside, a country outside my father’s kingdom of the earth.”

dhruvo ’pi yat-prasādena
yayau sarvottamaṁ padam
tasmai namo bhagavate
praṇata-kleśa-hāriṇe 6.9.24

“I offer my respectful obeisances to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who removes the sufferings of the surrendered souls and who mercifully gave to Dhruva Mahārāja the best of all abodes.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
ānartam ānataṁ dīnaṁ
bhagavān dīna-vatsalaḥ
prasannaḥ śrī-mukhenāha
megha-gambhīrayā girā 6.9.25

Śrī Nārada said: “Then the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is kind to the downtrodden and who was pleased at heart, with His glorious mouth spoke words deep like thunder to poor Anarta, who was bowing down before Him.”

śrī-bhagavān uvāca
anyā na medinī loke
kiṁ kartavyaṁ mayā nṛpa
sva-vacas tad ṛtaṁ kartuṁ
tvad-bhaktyā paritośitaḥ 6.9.26

The Blessed Lord said: “I am pleased by Your devotion, but I I do not have another earth in this universe to give you. O king, what should I do to grant the boon you ask?”

tasmād vai svasya lokasya
vaikuṇṭhasya parantapa
bhū-khaṇḍaṁ yojana-śataṁ
dadāmi vimalaṁ śubham 6.9.27

“Therefore, O mighty one, I will place on this earth an eight-hundred mile portion of my pure and glorious Vaikuṇṭha world.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
ity uktvānārta-nṛpatiṁ
bhagavān bhakta-vatsalaḥ
vaikuṇṭhāc ca samutpatya
bhū-khaṇḍaṁ śata-yojanam
cakraṁ sudarśanaṁ dhṛtvā
samudre bhīma-nādini
dadhāra bhagavān devas
tasyopari videha-rāṭ 6.9.28-29

Śrī Nārada said: “O king of Videha, after speaking these words, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who loves His devotees, took His Sudarśana-cakra, lifted up an eight-hundred mile portion of Vaikuṇṭha, and placed it in the ocean filled with fearful sounds.”

ānarto lakśa-varśāntaṁ
tatra rājyaṁ cakāra ha
putra-pautra-samāyukto
rājan vaikuṇṭha-sampadam 6.9.30

“O king, in this way, after ten thousand years Anarta attained a kingdom with all the opulences of Vaikuṇṭha and a dynasty with many noble sons and grandsons.”

idaṁ śrutvātha śaryātiḥ
pitā vai vismito ’bhavat
ānarto nāma deśo ’bhūd
ānartasya prasādataḥ 6.9.31

“By the Lord’s mercy that country was named Anarta after him. When he heard all this, Anarta’s father, Śaryāti, became astonished.”

revatas tasya putro ’bhūc
chrī-śailasya gireḥ sutam
samutpatya sva-hastābhyāṁ
ānarteśu nyapātayat 6.9.32

“Anarta’s son was named Revata. Revata lifted the mountain that was Mount Śaila’s son and placed it in the country of Anarta.”

so ’bhūd revata-nāmnāpi
raivato nāma parvataḥ
kuśasthalīṁ vinirmāya
rājyaṁ kṛtvātha revataḥ 6.9.33

“Revata. That mountain was named Raivata after him. Building a great capitol city named Kuśasthalī, Revata ruled the kingdom of Anarta.”

Chapter 10 - In the Description of the Gomatī River, the Glories of Cakra-tīrtha

śrī-nārada uvāca
itthaṁ mayā te kathitaṁ
dvārakāgama-kāraṇam
sarva-pāpa-haraṁ puṇyaṁ
kiṁ bhūyaḥ śrotum icchasi 6.10.1

Śrī Nārada said: “Thus I have told to you the story of how Dvārakā came to the earth, a sacred story that remnoves all sins. What more do you wish to hear?”

śrī-bahulāśva uvāca
sarva-tīrtha-mayī bhūmir
dvārakā nagarī śubhā

“A great sage named Trita, who was peaceful at heart and a devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa, went on a pilgrimage to the land of Anarta.”

dṛśṭvā śubhaṁ saraḥ snātvā

“Seeing a beautiful lake there, Trita bathed and then worshiped Lord Kṛṣṇa. In his worship he used a great and very beautiful conchshell.”

corayām āsa kakśivāṁs

“Whoever has stolen my conchshell will become a conch.” Cursed in this way, Kakśivān at once became a conch.”

tat-pādayor nipatitaḥ

“Kakśivān fell at Trita’s feet and begged, “Save me!” Trita was at once pacified. Still, he said, “Fool, what have you done? Because you have stolen, now you must suffer the sinful reaction. My words will not go in vain.”

bhaja śrī-kṛṣṇa-pādābjaṁ

“O king, saying, “Worship Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet, and He will release you from this and give you liberation,” the great sage Trita left.”

sarovare nipatitaḥ
kakśivān chaṇkha-rūpa-dhṛk
pravadan kṛṣṇa kṛśṇṣti

“Kakśivān, now in the form of a conch, fell into the lake. For a hundred years he stayed there, all the time chanting “Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!”

paripūrṇatamaḥ sākśād
bhagavān bhakta-vatsalaḥ
āgatya sarasas-tīraṁ
ma bhaiśṭety abhayaṁ dadau 6.10.9

“The perfect Supreme Personality of Godhead, who dearly loves His devotees, came to that lake and, saying, “Don’t be afraid, reassured Kakśivān.”

taṁ megha-nāda-gambhīraṁ
giraṁ śrutvā jalecaraḥ
cukrośa pāhi pāhīti
deva-deva jagat-pate 6.10.10

“Hearing the Lord’s words deep as thunder, the conch Kakśivān called out, “Lord of lords! Master of the universes! Save me! Save me!”

bhujagendra-bhoga-rucā
bhujena bhagavān prabhuḥ
śaṇkhaṁ bhaktaṁ gajam iva
projjahāra dayā-paraḥ 6.10.11

“Extending an arm splendid like a great serpent’s coil, the merciful Supreme Personality of Godhead lifted the devotee conch, who was as large as a great elephant.”

tadaiva divya-rūpo ’bhūc
chaṇkha-rūpaṁ vihāya saḥ
kṛtāñjalir hariṁ natvā
stutiṁ cakre tadā ca saḥ 6.10.12

“His conch form gone, Kakśivān, now in the splendid form of a demigod, bowed down before the Lord and with folded hands spoke many prayers.”

śrī-kakśivān uvāca
vāsudeva namas te ’stu
govinda puruśottama
dīna-vatsala dīneśa

Śrī Kakśivān said: “O Vāsudeva, O Govinda, O Supreme Person, O Supreme Controller, O king of Dvārakā, O master of the poor and downtrodden, O Lord who loves the poor and downtrodden, I offer my respectful obeisances to You.”

dhruve dhruva-padaṁ dātre
prahlādasyārti-hāriṇe
gajasyoddhāriṇe tubhyaṁ
baler bali-vide namaḥ 6.10.14

“To You, who gave an eternal kingdom to Dhruva, removed Prahlāda’s sufferings, delivered a regal elephant, and accepted Bali’s offering, I offer my respectful obeisances.”

draupadī-cira-santāna-
kāriṇe haraye namaḥ
garāgni-vana-vāsebhyaḥ
pāṇḍavānāṁ sahāyine 6.10.15

“To You, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who made Draupadī’s garment endless and rescued the Pāṇḍavas from poison, fire, and exile in the forest, I offer my respectful obeisances.”

yādava-trāṇa-kārtre ca
śakrād abhīra-rakśiṇe
guru-mātṛ-dvijānāṁ ca
putra-dātre namo namaḥ 6.10.16

“To You, who protected the Yādavas, protected the gopas from King Indra, and returned to Your mother, guru, and a brāhmaṇa the sons they had lost, I offer my respectful obeisances.”

jarāsandha-nirodhārta-
nṛpānāṁ mokśa-kāriṇe
nṛgasyoddhāriṇe sākśāt-
sudāmne dainya-hāriṇe 6.10.17

“To You, who rescued the kings suffering in Jarāsandha’s prison, delivered King Nṛga, and ended Sudāmā’s poverty, I offer my respectful obeisances.”

vāsudevāya kṛśṇṣya
namaḥ saṇkarśaṇāya ca
pradyumnāyāniruddhāya
catur-vyūhāya te namaḥ 6.10.18

“To You, who are the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Kṛṣṇa, I offer my respectful obeisances. To You, who are the Catur-vyūha: Lord Vāsudeva, Lord Saṇkarśaṇa, Lord Pradyumna, and Lord Aniruddha, I offer my respectful obeisances.”

tvam eva mātā ca pitā tvam eva
tvam eva bandhuś ca sakhā tvam eva
tvam eva vidyā draviṇaṁ tvam eva

“You are my mother. You are my father, You are my relative. You are my friend. You are my wisdom. You are my wealth. O Lord of Lords, You are my all.”

śrī-nārada uvāca
evaṁ stutvā hariṁ rājan

vimāna-varam āsthāya

Śrī Nārada said: “O king, after offering these prayers, Kakśivān, now filled with love, entered a spiritual airplane and, as the Yādavas looked on, without any trouble flew to the world of Lord Viṣṇu, filling the ten directions with light as he went.”

“O king of Mithilā, this very sacred place, where Lord Kṛṣṇa delivered a conch, is known as Śaṇkhoddhāra-tīrtha.”

Chapter 13 - The Glories of Prabhāsa-tīrtha, the Sarasvatī River, etc.

śrī-nārada uvāca
prabhāsasyāpi māhātmyaṁ
śṛṇu rājan mahā-mate
sarva-pāpa-haraṁ puṇyaṁ
tejasāṁ vardhanaṁ param 6.13.1

Śrī Nārada said: “O noble-hearted king, please hear the story of Prabhāsa-tīrtha, a story that removes all sins and grants gret power.”

godāvaryāṁ gurau siṁhe
hara-kśetre ca kumbhage
ravigrahe kurukśetre
kāśyāṁ candragrahe tathā

prabhāse ca dine dine 6.13.2-3

“By bathing and giving charity at Prabhāsa-tīrtha one attains a pious result a hundred times greater than the result attained by bathing and giving charity at the Godāvarī river when Jupiter is in Leo, at Haridwar when Jupiter is in Aquarius, at Kurukśetra during a solar eclipse, or at Vārāṇasī during a lunar eclipse.”

yatra snātvā dakśa-śāpād
gṛhīto yakśmaṇoḍurāṭ
vimuktaḥ kilbiśāt sadyo
bheje bhūyaḥ kalodayam 6.13.4

“By bathing at Prabhāsa-tīrtha, the moon-god became free of the disease he obtained by Dakśa’s curse, although he did show his phases again afterward.”

mahā-puṇyatamā rājan
yatra pratyak sarasvatī
tasyāṁ snātvā naraḥ pāpī
sākśād brahmamayo bhavet 6.13.5

“O king, in that place the Sarasvatī river flows in a western course. A sinner who bathes in the Sarasvatī there attains a spiritual body.”

tat-tīre vartate rājan
nāmnā vai bodha-pippalam
kṛśṇṣna yatroddhavāya
dattaṁ bhāgavataṁ śubham 6.13.6

“On the shore of the Sarasvatī is a place named Bodha-pippala, where Lord Kṛṣṇa gave the beautiful Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam to Uddhava.”

taṁ natvābhyarcya vidhivat
spṛśṭvā śrī-bodha-pippalam
śṛṇoti yo bhāgavataṁ
purāṇaṁ brahma-saṁhitam
ślokārdhaṁ śloka-pādaṁ vā
maunī niyata-mānasaḥ
tasya pāṇau bhaved rājan
vaiśṇaṣaṁ paramaṁ padam 6.13.7-8

“A person who is silent, controls his mind, visits, properly worships, and touches sacred Bodha-pippala, and hears the sacred Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam Purāṇa, or even only half a stanza of it, or even only a single line of a stanza, in Bodha-pippala, finds the supreme abode of Lord Viṣṇu is placed in his hand.”

prauśṭha-padyāṁ pūrṇimāyāṁ
hema-siṁha-samanvitam
dadāti yo bhāgavataṁ
sa yāti paramāṁ gatim 6.13.9

“A person who on the full-moon day of the month of Bhādra (August- September) gives a gift of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam resting on a golden throne attains the supreme destination.”

purāṇaṁ na śrutaṁ yais tu
śrīmad-bhāgavataṁ kvacit
teśāṁ vṛthā janma gataṁ
narāṇāṁ bhūmi-vāsinām 6.13.10

“living on the earth. In this world they who have never heard Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam have wasted their lives.”

yair na śrutaṁ bhāgavataṁ purāṇaṁ
nārādhito yaiḥ puruśaḥ purāṇaḥ
hūtaṁ mukhe naiva dharāmarāṇāṁ
teśāṁ vṛthā janma gataṁ narāṇām 6.13.11

“They who have not heard Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, worshiped the ancient Supreme Personality of Godhead, or fed the brāhmaṇas, have wasted their lives.”

dvāravatyāṁ tīrtha-rājaṁ
gomatī-sindhu-maṇgalam
yatra snātvā naro yāti
vaikuṇṭhaṁ vimalaṁ padam 6.13.12

“A person who bathes in Dvārakā at the auspicious place where the Gomatī flows into the ocean, a place that is the king of holy places, attains the sacred supreme abode of Vaikuṇṭha.”

śatāśvamedhajaṁ puṇyaṁ
gaṇgā-sāgara-saṇgame
tat-sahasra-guṇaṁ proktaṁ
gomatī-sindhu-saṇgame 6.13.13

“The pious result obtained by bathing at the place where the Gomatī flows into the ocean is a thousand times greater than the pious result obtained at the place where the Gaṇgā flows into the ocean.”

atraivodāharantīmaṁ
itihāsaṁ purātanam
yasya śravaṇa-mātreṇa
pāpa-tāpāt pramucyate 6.13.14

“The wise tell an ancient story about this holy place. Simply by hearing it one becomes free from the sufferings brought by many sins.”

āsīd gajāhvaye vaiśyo
rāja-mārga-patiḥ paraḥ
mahā-gaurava-samyukto
nidhīśo dhanado yathā 6.13.15

“In Hastināpura there was a great, generous, respectful, and very wealthy merchant.”

veśyā-prasaṇga-nirato
vīta-gośṭhī-viśāradaḥ
dyūta-krīḍaṇakāsakto
lobha-moha-madānvitaḥ 6.13.16

“He greedy, passionate, bewildered, fond of visiting prostitutes, expert at speaking amorous flattery, and addicted to gambling.”

mṛśā-vādī mahā-duśṭaḥ
ku-karma-nirataḥ sadā
brāhmaṇebhyo na pitṛbhyo
na devebhyo dhanaṁ dadau 6.13.17

“He was a lier and a rogue. He always acted badly. He never gave charity to the brāhmaṇas, pitās, or demigods.”

hareḥ kathāṁ prekśya dūrād
dūraṁ vai niryayau tvaram
pitroḥ sevāpi na kṛtā
na putrebhyo dhanaṁ dadau 6.13.18

“When from afar he was aware of discussions about the Supreme Personality of Godhead, he quickly ran far away. He did not serve his parents. He gave no money to his children.”

tyaktvā bhāryāṁ sa bhinno ’bhūd
dhanāḍhyo durmatiḥ khalaḥ
veśyā-prasaṇgāt tasyāpi
dhanārdhaṁ prakśayaṁ gatam 6.13.19

“This wicked, evil-hearted, and very wealthy person left his wife. By again and again visiting prostitutes he lost half his wealth.”

ardhaṁ tu taskarair nītaṁ
kiñcit pṛthvyāṁ gataṁ svataḥ
puṇyena vardhate lakśmīḥ
pāpena kśiyate dhruvam 6.13.20

“The half that remained was stolen by thieves. By performing pious deeds one becomes wealthy, and by performing sins one loses all his wealth.”

evaṁ hi nirdhano jāto
veśyā-sakto mahā-khalaḥ
tasmin gajāhvaye ramye
caurya-karma cakāra ha 6.13.21

“In this way he became penniless. Because he was addicted to visiting prostitutes, that rogue became a thief in the beautiful city of Hastināpura.”

caurya-karma prakurvantaṁ
baddhvā taṁ dāmibhir nṛpaḥ
deśān niḥsārayām āsa
śantanur nṛpatīśvaraḥ 6.13.22

“When he was caught stealing, the great king Śantanu had him bound with ropes and exiled from the country.”

vane ’pi nivasan so ’pi
jīva-hiṁsāṁ cakāra ha
samā dvādaśa-sāhasraṁ
na vavarśa yadā ghanaḥ 6.13.23

“the cloud. Living in the forest, he became a hunter. Then there was a great drought and for twelve thousand years there was no rain.”

paścimāṁ tu diśaṁ prāgād
vaiśyo durbhikśa-pīḍitaḥ
vane vai māritaḥ so ’pi
siṁhena tala-ghātataḥ 6.13.24

“Oppressed by the lack of food, he went to the west. In a forest there a lion clawed him to death.”

tadaiva yamadūtās taṁ
baddhvā pāśair adho-mukham
kaśāghātais tāḍayanto
ninyur mārgaṁ yamasya ca 6.13.25

“Then the Yamaūtas came, bound him with ropes, and, whipping him as they went, marched him, his face bowed, on the path to Yamarāja.”

atha kaścin mahān gṛdhro
māṁsaṁ tasya bhujasya ca
gṛhītvā khaṁ gataḥ sadyaḥ
khadaṁś cañcu-puṭena tam 6.13.26

“With its beak a great vulture suddenly bit off a part of his arm and flew into the sky, devouring it as it went.”

nirāmiśāḥ khagāś cānye
sāmiśaṁ jagmur āturāḥ
evaṁ kolāhale jāte
śaṇkha-cihnādibhiḥ kṛte 6.13.27

“The hungry vultures that had no meat pursued the vulture that had some. They attacked, scratching with their claws, and there was a great commotion.”

na jahau mukhato māṁsaṁ
paścimāśāṁ jagāma ha
tat-samenāpi gṛdhreṇa
tīkśna-tuṇḍena tāḍitāt 6.13.28

“Not letting the meat drop from its beak, the vulture flew to the west. Then another vulture, its equal in strength, attacked with its sharp beak.”

tan-mukhāt prapatan māṁsaṁ
gomatī-sindhu-saṇgame
tīrtha-plute tasya māṁse
vaiśyo ’yaṁ pātakī mahān

paraṁ dhāma harer yayau 6.13.29-31

“Then the meat dropped from the first vulture’s mouth and fell into the water at the place where the Gomatī flows into the ocean. Because of this, the sinful merchant became at once purified. He himself cut the ropes binding him and, as the Yamadūtas looked on, he became an effulgent four-armed resident of the spirituasl world. Entering a spiritual airplane, and illuminating all the directions as he went, he traveled to the spiritual abode of the Personality of Godhead.”

Chapter 14 - The Glories of Ratnākara, Raivata, and Kācala

tad-deha-sparśanāt sadyo

chinatti pāpa-paṭalaṁ
paraṁ mokśaṁ prayāti hi 6.14.5

“By seeing him even a sinner who by killing everyone in the world has become like a walking corpse becomes free of sin and attains liberation.”

raivatasyātha śailasya
māhātmyaṁ śṛṇu mānada
sarva-pāpa-haraṁ puṇyaṁ
mukti-bhukti-pradāyakam 6.14.6

“O respectful one, please hear the story of Mount Raivata’s glories, a sacred story that removes all sins, brings happiness in this life, and awards liberation at the end.”

gautamasya suto dhīmān
medhāvī nāma vaiśṇaṣaḥ
vindhyācale tapas tepe
varśānām ayutaṁ śatam 6.14.7

“There was a Vaiśṇṣva named Medhāvī, who was very intelligent and was the son of Gautama Muni. For one million years he performed austerities in the Vindyā mountains.”

taṁ draśṭum āgataḥ sākśād

apāntaratamas taṁ vai
śaśāpa krodha-pūritaḥ
satām abhakta pāpātman
sva-tapo-bala-garvitaḥ 6.14.9

“Filled with anger, Apāntaratama Muni cursed him, “Sinner, you have no devotion and respect for saintly persons. You are very proud of the power of your austerities.”

śailavat te sthitiś cātra
tvaṁ śailo bhava durmate
ity uktvātha gate sākśād
apāntaratame munau 6.14.10

medhāvī śailatāṁ prāptaḥ
śrī-śailasya suto ’bhavat
jāti-smaro mahā-buddhir
viṣṇu-bhakteḥ prabhāvataḥ 6.14.11

ekadā man-mukhāc chrutvā
māhātmyaṁ dvārakā-puraḥ
provāca so ’pi rājānaṁ
raivataṁ gaccha sa-tvaram 6.14.12

etasmiṁs tac corayituṁ
prayāte nṛpa-sattame
tat-pūrvāsmād ahaṁ prāptaḥ
śrī-śailasya pure nṛpa 6.14.17

kali-priyeṇāpi mayā
śrī-śailāya mahātmane

śrī-śailaḥ putra-mohena

putra-sneha-samākulaḥ
eko daivena datto ’yaṁ
na putrā bahavaś ca me 6.14.19-20

“Overcome with love for his son, Mount Śaila rebuked him, “Where will you go?” Then Mount Śaila went to regal Mount Sumeru and regal Mount Himālaya and, overcome with love for his son, said to them, “Destiny has given me only one son. I do not have many sons.”

taṁ hartum āgate rājñi
raivate vai mahā-bale

Chapter 15 - The Glories of Nṛga-kūpa and Gopī-bhūmi

tasmāt koṭi-guṇaṁ puṇyaṁ
dāna-tīrthe videha-rāṭ
māsam ekaṁ ca yat snānaṁ

“Yamarāja’s scribe, Citragupta, cannot understand how great is his pious benefit. The demigod Brahmā cannot describe the glories of Dāna-tīrtha.”

sarveśāṁ caiva dānānāṁ
aśva-dānaṁ paraṁ smṛtam
aśva-dānād gajasyāpi
gaja-dānād rathasya ca 6.19.33

“Giving a horse in charity is great charity. Still, giving an elephant is better than giving a horse and giving a chariot is better than giving an elephant.”

ratha-dānāt paraṁ rājan
bhūmi-dānaṁ viśiśyate
bhūmi-dānād anna-dānaṁ
mahā-dānaṁ prakathyate 6.19.34

“O king, giving land is better than giving a chariot, and giving food is better than giving land. Giving food is said be be great charity.”

anna-dāna-samaṁ dānaṁ
na bhūtaṁ na bhaviśyati

“There never has been nor will there ever be another charity as good as giving food. The demigods, sages, pitās and bhūtas are all pleased by a gift of food.”

dāna-tīrthe hy anna-dānaṁ

ṛṇa-trayaṁ vimucyātha
yāti viśṣoḥ paraṁ padam 6.19.36

“A noble-hearted person who gives charity at Dāna-tīrtha becomes free of the three debts and goes to the transcendental abode of Lord Viṣṇu.”

daśaiva mātṛke pakśe
rājendra daśa paitṛke
priyāyā daśa pakśe tu
puruśān uddharen naraḥ 6.19.37

catur-bhujā divya-rūpā
nāgarī-kṛta-ketanāḥ
sragvinaḥ pīta-vastrās te
prayānti hari-mandiram 6.19.38

bhagavan-mandirād rājann
uttarasyāṁ disi śrutaṁ
krośārdhe nṛpa-śārdūla
māyā-tīrthaṁ manoharam 6.19.39

“O tiger of kings, one mile north of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s palace is beautiful Māyā-tīrtha.”

virājate yatra nityaṁ
durgā durgati-nāśinī
siṁhārūḍhā bhadrakālī
caṇḍa-muṇḍa-vināśinī 6.19.40

“Goddess Durgā, who removes all troubles and kills the cruel and wicked, always stays there.”

syamantakaṁ samahārtuṁ
ṛkśa-rāja-bilaṁ gate
putre ca devakī devīṁ
pūjayām āsa sat-phalaiḥ 6.19.41

“In this place, when her son had gone to Jāmbavān’s cave to recover the Syamantaka jewel, Devakī worshiped Goddess Durgā and offered her the best of fruits.”

tadājagāma priyayā
sa-maṇir bhagavān hariḥ

“When Lord Kṛṣṇa returned from that cave with Satyabhāmā and the Syamantaka jewel, Devakī considered that was the result of her worship at Māyā-tīrtha.”

Chapter 20 - In the Description of the Second Fort, the Glories of Indra-tīrtha, etc.

śrī-nārada uvāca

indra-tīrthaṁ mahā-puṇyaṁ
kāma-daṁ siddhi-dāyakam 6.20.1

Śrī Nārada said: “O king of Videha, at the eastern gate of the second fortress is sacred Indra-tīrtha, which grants perfection and fulfills all desires.”

tatra snātvā naro rājann
indralokaṁ prayāti hi
ihaiva candra-sādṛśyaṁ
vaibhavaṁ prāpyate naraḥ 6.20.2

yadā satrājitenāpi
pūjito ’bhūt syamantakaḥ 6.20.3

tatra snātvā padmarāgaṁ
yo dadāti nṛpeśvara
sūrya-prabha-vimānena
sūryalokaṁ prayāti hi 6.20.4

“A person who bathes there and gives a ruby in charity flies to the sun planet in an airplane effulgent like the sun.”

tathā vai paścime dvāre
brahma-tīrthaṁ viśiśyate

indraloke padaṁ dhṛtvā

“At the western gate is Brahma-tīrtha. Please here the pious result attained by one who bathes there and gives charity of pāyasa in a golden cup. Such a person is very wise. Even if he has killed his father, his mother, his guru, a brāhmaṇa, or a cow, or even if he has committed many very terrible sins, he attains a form like that of Lord Brahmā and, riding in an airplane effulgent as the moon, goes to Brahmaloka.”

tathā vai uttare dvāre

devatā munayaḥ sarve
tathā sāptarśayaḥ pare
vasanti yatra vaideha
tathā sarve marud-gaṇāḥ 6.20.9

“All the demigods and sages, the seven ṛśis, all the Maruts, and many others all reside in that place.”

nīlalohita-liṇgaṁ tu
yatra sampūjya yatnataḥ
aiśvaryam atulaṁ lebhe
rāvaṇo loka-rāvaṇaḥ 6.20.10

“At this place the demon Rāvaṇa, who made the entire world scream with grief, worshiped the liṇga of Lord Śiva and attained incomparable power and opulence.”

kailāsasyāpi yātrāyāṁ
yat phalaṁ labhate nṛpa
tasmāc chata-guṇaṁ puṇyaṁ
nīlalohita-darśanāt 6.20.11

“O king, by seeing the liṇga of Lord Śiva there one attains a pious result a hundred times greater than the result attained by going on pilgrimage to Mount Kailāsa.”

nīlalohita-kuṇḍe vai
snāto yas tri-dinaṁ naraḥ
sa yāti śiva-lokākhyaṁ
pāpāyuta-yuto ’pi hi 6.20.12

“A person who visits for three days and bathes in Nīlalohita-kuṇḍa goes to Śivaloka, even though he may have committed many millions of sins.”

sapta-sāmudrakaṁ nāma
tīrthaṁ yatra virājate
tatra snātvā naraḥ pāpi
pāpa-saṇghaiḥ pramucyate 6.20.13

“There also is Sapta-sāmudraka-tīrtha. A sinner who bathes there is freed from a great host of sins.”

saptānāṁ ca samudrāṇāṁ

viśṣur viriñco giriśa
indro vāyur yamo raviḥ
parjanyo dhanadaḥ somaḥ
kśitir agnir apāṁ patiḥ

“He attains the pious result of bathing in the seven oceans. O king, Lord Viṣṇu, Brahmā, Śiva, Indra, Vāyu, Yama, Sūrya, Indra, Kuvera, Candra, Kśiti, Agni, and Varuṇa always stay on the shores of Sapta-sāmudraka-tīrtha.”

sāpta-koṭīni tīrthāni
brahmāṇḍe yāni kāni ca
sarvāṇi tatra tiśṭhanti
sapta-sāmudrake nṛpa 6.20.16

Chapter 21 - In the Description of the Third Fort, the Glories of Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha

hanumān añjanī-sutaḥ 6.21.1

chrī-kṛṣṇa-hṛdayo mahān 6.21.7

tasya darśana-mātreṇa
naro yāti kṛtārthatām
śṛṇu rājan bahir durgāt
tīrthaṁ piṇḍārakaṁ smṛtam 6.21.8

śṛṇutād rāja-sattama
yasya smaraṇa-mātreṇa
mahā-pāpāt pramucyate 6.21.9

“O best of kings, please hear the glories of Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha. Simply by hearing its glories one becomes free from the greatest sin.”

artha-siddhyor iva dvāre
raivatādri-samudrayoḥ
madhye piṇḍāraka-kśetraṁ
tīrthānāṁ tīrtham uttamam 6.21.10

“As if it were standing between perfection and the goal of life, Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha, the best of holy places, stands between Mount Raivata and the ocean.”

kratu-rājaṁ rājasūyaṁ
yadu-rājo mahā-balaḥ
cakāra yatra vaideha
pairpūrṇatamājñayā 6.21.11

“By the perfect Supreme Personality of Godhead’s order, powerful Ugrasena, the king of the Yādavas, performed a Rājasūya-yajña, the best of yajñas, at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha.”

sarvāṇi yatra tīrthāni
samāhutāni sarvataḥ
nivāsaṁ cakrire rājann
ugrasena-kratūttame 6.21.12

“When they were called, all holy places came to King Ugrasena’s Rājasūya-yajña.”

tena piṇḍārakaṁ nāma
sarva-tīrthasya piṇḍataḥ
tatra snātvā naraḥ sadyo
rājasūya-phalaṁ labhet 6.21.13

“Because all the holy places converged (piṇḍ) at that place, it was named Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha. A person who bathes there attains the result of performing a Rājasūya-yajña.”

“A person who, carefully following religious vows, stays there for three days, bathes there, gives gold in charity to the brāhmaṇas, and humbly bows down before them, becomes a great saintly king in this world. Again and again he hears the poets chanting his glories.”

suvarṇa-ratna-vastrādyaiḥ
sucandra-vadanaiḥ paraiḥ
strī-saṇghaiḥ sevito nityaṁ
hṛśṭa-puśṭo mahā-balaḥ 6.21.16

“He is dressed in costly garments and decorated with gold and jewels. Many beautiful moon-faced wives serve him always. He is powerful and and happy.”

aśva-hreśaiḥ samanvitam 6.21.17

“Day and night at the gateway to his palace many drums are sounded, many regal elephants trumpet, and many horses neigh.”

virājate rāja-saṇghaiḥ
prekśayan prāṇganājiram
ratna-prāsāda-nicayaṁ
dhvaja-maṇḍala-maṇḍitam 6.21.18

“Surrounded by his entourage, he is splendidly manifested in his many jewel palaces decorated with flags and enveloping charming gardens and courtyards.”

alaṇkaroti tad-dvāraṁ
maṇḍitaṁ maṇḍaleśvaraiḥ 6.21.19

“The entrance to his palace is decorated with regal attendants and with swarms of bees struck by the ears of intoxicated elephants.”

“Without bathing at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha there is no happiness. Without bathing at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha there are no pious deeds. Without bathing at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha there is no religion. Without bathing at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha there is no protection.”

“Without bathing at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha one cannot be a yogī. One who bathes at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha becomes a yogī. One who bathes at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha enjoys all happiness. One who bathes at Piṇḍāraka-tīrtha is never diseased.”

dvāravatīṁ mādhava-māsa-madhye
pradakśiṇī-kṛtya namaskaroti
sarvā ihāmūtra ca siddhayo ’pi
vaideha tat-pāṇi-tale bhavanti 6.21.23

“A person who in the month of Mādhava (April-May) circumambulates Dvārakā finds that in both this life and the next all perfections rest in the palm of his hand.”

tīrthāpluto-’dhaḥ-śayanaḥ śuciś ca
maunī vratī vā yava-bhojanena
ārabhya caitrīṁ kila paurṇamāsīṁ
yo mādhavīm etya karoti yātrām
tat-puṇya-saṇkhyaṁ gadituṁ na śakyas
catur-mukho vedamayo vidhātā
yo megha-dhāraṁ gaṇayet kadācit
kālena puṇyāni na kṛṣṇa-puryāḥ 6.21.24-25

“A person who, following many vows, including a vow of silence and a vow of eating only barley, goes on a pilgrimage to Dvārakā and bathes and sleeps at Dvārakā on the full-moon day of the month of Caitra (March-April) or the full-moon day of the month of Mādhava (April-May) attains piety so great even the demigod Brahmā, who is the creator of the planets and the master of Vedas, cannot measure it. If one could somehow, with a great effort and after a long time, count the drops of rain that fall during the monsoon rains, still he could never measure the piety one gains by visiting Lord Kṛṣṇa’s city of Dvārakā.”

yathā tithīnāṁ hari-vāsaraṁ ca
yathā hi śeśaḥ phaṇināṁ phaṇīndraḥ
yathā garutmān divi pakśiṇāṁ ca
yathā purāṇeśu ca bhārataṁ ca
yathā hi deveśu ca deva-devaḥ
śrī-vāsudevo yadu-deva-devaḥ
tathā purī kśetra-samasta-madhye
dvāravatī puṇyavatī praśastā 6.21.26-27

“As Ekādaśī is glorious among sacred days, as Lord Śeśa, the king of serpents, is glorious among serpents, as Garuḍa is glorious among birds in the sky, as the Mahābhārata is glorious among the Purāṇas, and as Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the master of the demigods and the Yādavas, is glorious among all deities, so sacred Dvārakā is glorious among all sacred places.”

aho ’ti-dhanyā yadu-maṇḍalībhir
virājate bhūmi-tale manoharā
vaikuṇṭha-līlādhikṛtā kuśasthalī
yathā taḍidbhir jaladāvalir divi 6.21.28

“Beautiful and glorious Dvārakā, in this world the home of the Yādavas and the pastimes of Vaikuṇṭha, is splendid like monsoon clouds and lightning.”

yatraiva sākśāt puruśaḥ pareśvaro
dhṛtvā catur-vyūham alaṁ virājate
yas tūgrasenāya dadau nṛpeśatāṁ
kṛśṇṣya tasmai haraye namo namaḥ 6.21.29

“I offer my respectful obeisances to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who expands as the catur-vyūha, and who gave the Yādavas’ kingdom to Ugrasena.”

yadā sva-lokaṁ bhagavān gamiśyati
samplāvayiśyaty atha tāṁ tadārṇavaḥ
vaideha divyaṁ hari-mandiraṁ vinā
tasmin nivāsaṁ bhagavān kariśyati 6.21.30

“When Lord Kṛṣṇa returns to His own abode, the ocean will flood Dvārakā City. O king of Videha, the Lord will not reside then in that palace on the earth.”

śṛṇvanti tatraiva kalau jala-dhvaniṁ
kṛśṇokṣam itthaṁ satataṁ dine dine
bhaved avidyo yadi vā sa-vidyo
yo brāhmaṇo vai sa tu māmakī tanuḥ 6.21.31

“In the Kali-yuga if a person, either unlettered or a learned brāhmaṇa, hears Lord Kṛṣṇa’s voice in the sound of the Dvārakā ocean’s waves, then Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself declares, “That person represents Me.”

bhūtvātha vipro ’bdhi-tatād agādhaṁ
gatvā gṛhītvā pratimāṁ parasya
kṛtvā pratiśṭhāṁ ca vidhāya saudhaṁ
kariśyate sthāpanam arka eśaḥ 6.21.32

“A brāhmaṇa who establishes a Deity form of the Lord in a great temple by the ocean at Dvārakā becomes glorious like the sun.”

śrī-dvārakā-nātham iti svarūpaṁ
paśyanti ye bhakta-janāḥ kalau yuge
gacchanti te viṣṇu-padaṁ nṛ-deva
yogīśvarāṇām api durlabhaṁ yat 6.21.33

“In Kali-yuga the devotees who gaze on the Deity of Lord Kṛṣṇa, the master of Dvārakā, go to Lord Viṣṇu’s transcendental abode, which even the greatest yogīs cannot attain.”

idaṁ mayā te kathitaṁ nṛ-deva
māhātmyam etat kila kṛṣṇa-puryāḥ
śṛṇoti yaḥ śrāvayate ca bhaktyā
śrī-dvārakā-vāsa-phalaṁ labhet saḥ 6.21.34

“O king, thus I have spoken to you the glories of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s Dvārakā City. A person who with devotion hears or causes others to hear these glories of Dvārakā attains the spiritual result of having lived there.”
